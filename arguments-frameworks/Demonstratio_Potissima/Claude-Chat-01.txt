Mereological Irreducibility (Part I, #8): This is philosophically contested. Gödel's Scope (Part II, #3-6): Gödel's theorems apply to formal systems of a specific type—roughly, those capable of expressing arithmetic. Three problems:

Is physical reality a "formal system" in the relevant technical sense? It's not clear.
The theorem concerns provability, not truth or consistency. A system can be consistent without being able to prove its own consistency.
The jump from "can't prove consistency from within" to "requires an external intellect" assumes intellectualist premises about what grounds consistency.
Universal Consistency (Part II, #4): The claim that physical reality's consistency is an "empirical fact" is too strong. We observe local consistency. Act/Potency Framework (Part III): This presupposes Aristotelian metaphysics. Alternative frameworks (Humean, process philosophy, structural realism) don't accept these categories. The argument would need to first establish this metaphysical framework against rivals.
