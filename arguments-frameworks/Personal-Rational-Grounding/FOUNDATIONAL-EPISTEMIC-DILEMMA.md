# PRG: The Foundational Epistemic Dilemma

Date: 2026-11-28
Status: Owner-directed research addendum, pending validation.
Parent: [The Necessity of Personal Rational Grounding](WORK-PACKAGE.md).

## Owner insight

The grounding inquiry resolves into two necessary options: epistemic nihilism or epistemic objectivism. Qualify these terms at the foundational level to avoid conflating ordinary epistemic objectivism with a commitment to universal rational explicability.

- Foundational epistemic objectivism: objective truth and logical normativity obtain, and ultimate actuality possesses sufficient rational explanation, including the determination of genuine contingencies.
- Foundational epistemic nihilism: ultimate rational explanatory sufficiency fails; at least one fundamental contingent determination lacks sufficient reason. This is a proposed technical usage, not a claim that all adherents deny knowledge, objective truth, or local rational inquiry.

## Formal structure

Let R mean ultimate rational explanatory sufficiency, C genuine contingent actuality, and P personal rational agency.

1. Rational inquiry presupposes objective truth and logical normativity.
2. The epistemic endeavor seeks sufficient rational explanation.
3. R ∨ ¬R (excluded middle, with R held fixed).
4. Where C obtains, ¬R entails foundational explanatory insufficiency; where the deficiency consists in a contingent determination lacking sufficient reason, it is contrastive arbitrariness.
5. The PRG conditional (C ∧ R) → P follows if the determination taxonomy is exhaustive and personal reason is explanatorily adequate.
6. Consequently C → (P ∨ ¬R), equivalently (C ∧ ¬P) → ¬R, under those premises.

The excluded-middle dichotomy is exact at the level of R. The resulting alternatives P and ¬R are not necessarily mutually exclusive: personal agency could itself fail the sufficiency criterion. Establishing a non-contingent unified personal source requires the additional grounding premises in the parent WP.

## Epistemic significance

A worldview accepting ultimate brute contingency employs rationality epistemologically while accepting arbitrariness ontologically. It can retain local rational inquiry, but relinquishes ultimate explanatory sufficiency. Unknown reasons must be distinguished from reasons that do not exist. Agnosticism about R is an epistemic attitude, not a third truth value for R.

The proposed organizing thesis is that consistently extending epistemic objectivism to ultimate ontological grounding entails personal rational intentionality. The argument aims to establish transcendental necessity under the conditions of a rationally sufficient epistemic endeavor, rather than merely comparative explanatory preference.

- Test the transition from objective truth and rational normativity to ultimate ontological rational sufficiency; ordinary epistemic objectivism alone does not entail R.
- Test whether partial explanation, necessary impersonal grounding, or unknown grounding undermines the proposed terminology or classification.
- Preserve the distinction between absolute and contrastive arbitrariness, and apply identical explanatory standards to personal and impersonal alternatives.
