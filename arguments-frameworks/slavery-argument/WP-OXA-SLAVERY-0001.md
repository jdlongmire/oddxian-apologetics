## 2. Placement and baseline

## 3. Thesis

God's regulation of an institution does not, by itself, establish moral endorsement. Biblical law accommodates and restricts fallen institutions while its governing moral principles remain grounded in God's nature and love of God and neighbor. The Exodus, release provisions, prophetic appeals, and New Testament teachings offer affirmative evidence that liberation is a biblical good. Leviticus 25:44–46 remains a substantial interpretive challenge.

## 4. Research questions

1. What do permission, authorization, regulation, accommodation, endorsement, and abolition each establish?
2. How do Deuteronomy 6:4–5, Leviticus 19:18 and 19:34, and Matthew 22:37–40 govern the moral interpretation?
3. What are the limits of the analogy to permitted divorce in Matthew 19:8?
4. What does the Exodus demonstrate, and what does it not establish universally?
5. How do inheritable foreign-slavery provisions coexist with the image of God and love of the sojourner?
6. What can and cannot be inferred from Ephesians 6:5–9, 1 Corinthians 7:21, and Philemon 8–21?
7. Does Scripture warrant an abolitionist trajectory, and at what confidence level?

## 5. Manuscript outline

I. **Introduction:** Define the contested terms; state the strongest slavery objection.

II. **Creation and communion:** Genesis 1:26–27; 2:18–24; John 17:24; 1 John 4:8. Separate direct claims from synthesis.

III. **Love as meta-law:** Deuteronomy 6:4–5; Leviticus 19:18, 34; Matthew 22:37–40; Romans 13:8–10. Label “meta-law” as analytical terminology.

IV. **Fall and accommodation:** Genesis 3–4; Psalm 130:3–4; Matthew 19:8; Romans 2:4; Noahic covenant. Qualify the immediate-judgment counterfactual.

V. **God's demonstrated disposition:** Exodus 3:7–8; 20:2; Deuteronomy 15:12–15; Jeremiah 34:8–17; Isaiah 58:6. Distinguish Israel's liberation from universal abolition legislation.

VI. **Slavery law and release:** Exodus 21:2, 16, 20–21, 26–27; Leviticus 25:10, 39–46; Deuteronomy 23:15–16. Address the asymmetry between Hebrew and foreign servitude and disputed applicability of protections.

VII. **New Testament evidence:** Luke 4:18–19; 1 Corinthians 7:21; Ephesians 6:5–9; Philemon 8–21; Galatians 3:28; 1 Timothy 1:10. Keep spiritual and civil liberation distinct.

VIII. **Strongest objections:** Permanent inheritable property; the centurion; Onesimus; household codes; silence; historical religious defenses.

IX. **Synthesis:** Identify what is established, inferred, and unresolved.

## 6. Source protocol

- Scripture: ESV with verified verse-level references and quotation fidelity.
- Original languages: consult primary Hebrew/Greek and lexical resources before asserting lexical conclusions.
- History: seek original ANE, patristic, papal, and other relevant primary records.
- Secondary references: verify bibliographic facts and page numbers; label primary sources obtained through secondary accounts “as cited in.”
- Confidence: HIGH (verified primary), MEDIUM (page-specific secondary), LOW (paraphrase/tertiary), UNCERTAIN (unresolved).
- Explicitly engage Leviticus 25:44–46 and the contested scopes of Deuteronomy 23:15–16 and 1 Corinthians 7:21.
