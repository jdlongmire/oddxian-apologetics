## Analysis

### Objection 1: "Energy source is pervasive, water can't provide required cooling"

This misunderstands the mechanism. The model doesn't claim water *cools* mantle-generated heat. It claims the mechanism **bypasses mantle shear entirely**:

**From paper (Section 3.3):**
- Blocks move by hydroplaning on thin water films at **shallow depths** (upper crust to ~20 km)
- The mantle remains largely **passive** - it doesn't flow at catastrophic velocities
- Energy source is **gravitational potential** + **pressure gradients**, not mantle convection

The quantitative force balance (Section 4.1) shows:
- 0.1° slope on 800×1000×35 km block produces ~1.3×10^18 N driving force
- Friction under pore pressure collapse yields ~3×10^16 N resistance
- Net force exceeds friction by factor of ~70

Heat isn't generated "pervasively" through deep mantle viscous shear - it's dissipated in water at shallow depths where heat capacity is 4x higher and cooling is efficient. Result: ~7 W/m² flux and ~1 K warming, not hundreds of K.

### Objection 2: "Interior water is in the form of mineral hydrates"

The paper explicitly addresses this (Section 2.1):

> "The critical insight is that ringwoodite hydration **requires water to have existed as free liquid before being carried downward**. Oceanic crust becomes hydrated through contact with liquid water at or near the surface... Therefore, every kilogram of H₂O now sequestered at depth represents water that was **once mobile** in the crustal or surface hydrosphere."

The current hydrate state is **post-collapse sequestration**, not the pre-flood condition. The 1-3 ocean masses in transition zone minerals is forensic evidence *for* the model - it constrains where the mobile water went.
