### HIGH PRIORITY (Prevents Lingering Objections)

**1. Block-Driving Forces Vulnerability**
**Issue:** "Reviewers will still probe: Where the lateral slopes originated, Whether the pressure gradients are sustainable over 1000+ km"

**Current state:** Math is solid (70:1 force imbalance), but lacks dynamic evolution explanation

**Required additions:**
A. **1-paragraph defense** that slopes/gradients evolve dynamically during collapse
B. **Conceptual explanation** of where lateral slopes originated

**Location:** Section 4.1, after the quantitative force balance (line ~174)

**Draft addition:**

> These driving forces are not static but evolve dynamically during collapse. Lateral slopes originate from differential subsidence as hydraulic seals fail sequentially: basins underlain by failed seals subside rapidly while adjacent regions with intact seals remain elevated, creating local slopes of 0.1-1° over basin-scale distances. As collapse progresses, these slopes migrate and intensify. Pressure gradients similarly evolve as fluid migrates from high-pressure compartments (intact seals) toward low-pressure sinks (failed basins, surface breaches). The gradients are transient rather than sustained: they drive flow until pressures equilibrate locally, at which point new seal failures create fresh gradients elsewhere. The result is a spatially and temporally varying force field that drives blocks episodically rather than continuously, with motion concentrated in periods when local gradients are steep and damped when equilibration occurs. This dynamic evolution explains how forces remain sufficient over 1000+ km displacements despite being generated by local, transient processes.
