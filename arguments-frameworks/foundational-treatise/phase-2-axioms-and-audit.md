# Phase 2: Unified Axiom Set and Consistency Audit

**Treatise:** Triune Loving Communion: God's Good Decree from Creation to Perfected Communion. A Foundational Treatise.
**Status:** working draft for coordinator review
**Date:** 2026-11-01
**Scope of this document:** the unified axiom set (section 1), the derivation map from axioms to Part-level theses (section 2), and the consistency audit across the set (section 3), with items the audit cannot settle reserved for JD (section 4). The Appendix C broader-corpus registers are a separate Phase 2 deliverable and are not in this file.

**Method note.** Every axiom is drawn from the Phase 1 exegetical studies (cited as "study NN, finding N"), the five Phase 2 research notes (cited by note and section), or the work package itself (cited as "WP sec. N"). No axiom exceeds what its cited basis supports; where a needed premise is synthesis, the axiom is tagged synthesis. Claim tags and confidence levels follow the WP standard: Explicit text / Necessary inference / Theological synthesis / Philosophical conjecture, with HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / UNCERTAIN. Mediation limits recorded in the studies and notes (for example, BDB-level lexical work where HALOT was not consulted, and excerpt-level access to Maximus) are inherited by every axiom resting on them and are collected in section 4.

## 1. The unified axiom set

### Prolegomena: authority, boundary, method

**PRO.1 (The boundary of belonging).** There is a real and permanent division between a domain of things reserved to the LORD and a domain revealed and entrusted to his creatures forever, given for obedience.
Basis: study 04, finding 1. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH. Serves: Prolegomena, Part V.

**PRO.2 (The epistemic rule, as consequence not statement).** From "no morally sufficient reason for this evil is visible to us" it does not follow that there is no such reason; and the boundary text supplies no positive evidence that a reason exists in any particular case.
Basis: study 04, finding 6. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM. Serves: Prolegomena, Parts V, X.

**PRO.3 (Method).** Every non-explicit claim in the treatise shows its premises and its inference, by good and necessary consequence (WCF 1.6); observation, established fact, inference, hypothesis, and synthesis are kept distinct and tagged.
Basis: WP sec. 3, Track B. Tag: method axiom (not a doctrinal claim). Serves: all Parts.

### Part I: Theology proper. Eternal triune communion as the moral archetype

**I.1 (The two poles of divine affection).** God's regret and grief over human wickedness are real and moral in object, and neither asserts ignorance, error, or instability of character or counsel; the canon holds genuine divine affection in time together with no mutability of character, in a single chapter (1 Samuel 15) and across it (Numbers 23:19).
Basis: study 02, findings 1, 2, 3. Tag: Canonical synthesis, HIGH for the two poles as textual data; the metaphysical reconciliation is deferred to Part V and is not claimed by this axiom. Serves: Parts I, IV, V.

**I.2 (The archetype).** Triune loving communion, God's own eternal life of love, is the moral archetype of the created order: the pattern from which the treatise draws its account of the good, its definition of sin, and the shape of the consummated state.
Basis: WP sec. 1 and sec. 2A (governing thesis of the internal corpus); study 11, finding 6 (the consummation as communion); study 02, findings 1-2 (divine love and grief are real). Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM. The broader-corpus novelty check for this claim is a Phase 2 register task and has not been performed; the axiom is carried as the corpus's governing thesis, not as a verified originality claim. Serves: Parts I, IV, VIII, IX.

**I.3 (One divine will).** God's will is one and undivided; the distinction between his secret counsel and his revealed precept is a distinction of manner and of our apprehension, not two contrary wills in God.
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, sec. 3.3 (Calvin, Institutes 1.18.3, Beveridge, direct). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH within the treatise's confessional frame. Serves: Parts I, II, V.

### Part II: The eternal covenant and God's good decree

**II.1 (Domain of the working).** God works all things according to the counsel of his will; within the created totality the sentence itself defines (things in heaven and things on earth, Ephesians 1:10), the domain of his working admits no remainder outside his counsel.
Basis: study 06, findings 3, 4. Tag: Necessary inference from the discourse structure, HIGH. Serves: Parts II, V.

**II.2 (Domain, not mode).** Ephesians 1:11 does not specify the mode of God's working (its relation to secondary causes, creaturely intention, or evil as evil), states no per-event benevolent telos, and states no reprobation; the non-elect are absent from the sentence.
Basis: study 06, finding 5. Tag: Explicit text (verified absences in a fully inventoried sentence), HIGH. Serves: Parts II, V.

**II.3 (The governing premise, RP).** For those who love God, those called according to his purpose, all things are worked together toward the good, where the good is defined forward and Christologically as conformity to the Son and glorification; the beneficiaries are restricted to the called, and the good is eschatological, not temporal relief. RP does not depend on the Romans 8:28 textual variant: beneficiaries, comprehensiveness, and telos are identical on every attested reading; only the grammatical location of the agency claim varies, and on the least explicit construal the agency is carried by the immediate context and by Ephesians 1:11.
Basis: study 05, findings 1, 3, 5. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH (findings 1, 3); Necessary inference, HIGH (finding 5). Serves: Parts II, V, VIII.

**II.4 (No event-by-event indispensability).** No text in the anchor set asserts that each particular evil was necessary as an instrument, or that each event is itself good; the working of Romans 8:28 is toward an end and leaves the moral character of the events untouched.
Basis: study 05, finding 6. Tag: Explicit text (negative point), HIGH. Serves: Parts II, V.

**II.5 (Election).** God chose and predestined persons in Christ before the foundation of the world, on grounds exclusively on his own side (his pleasure, purpose, and the counsel of his will), with no human quality, act, or foreseen response named as a ground or condition; the sentence's form is corporate and Christ-located, and its objects are persons.
Basis: study 06, findings 1, 2, 7. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH (grounds and pretemporal dating); Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH (the individual entailment, finding 7). Serves: Part II.

**II.6 (The pactum salutis).** The eternal counsel of redemption is fittingly stated in covenantal form as the pactum salutis: the Father appointing the Son, stipulating his obedience unto death, and promising reward and headship; the Son undertaking suretyship for the elect; the Holy Spirit named explicitly in the statement. The pactum is a theological construction by collation from the appointment, giving, sending, obedience, and reward texts; it is formally distinct from the decree, not a second decree; it is one divine will appropriated distinctly to the persons, never two wills bargaining.
Basis: research note on the pactum salutis, secs. 0, 3.3, 4. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM for scriptural explicitness; HIGH for attestation in the Reformed tradition from 1638 onward. Serves: Part II.

**II.7 (Hebrews 13:20 as chain member).** The covenant whose blood was shed is the new covenant, and it is named eternal in the lexical sense of endless; the Father's raising of the Son was effected in virtue of that covenant blood and is the covenant's first executed consequence, rendered to the Mediator before it flows to the sheep. The verse is a member of the pactum's evidence chain, not its source: it names no covenanting parties in eternity and does not call the covenant beginningless.
Basis: study 10, findings 4, 5, 6, 7, 9. Tag: Necessary inference, MEDIUM-HIGH (findings 4, 7); Lexical verdict, HIGH (finding 6); Explicit text, HIGH (the absences, finding 9). Serves: Part II.

**II.8 (The decree chain).** Foreknowledge of persons, predestination to conformity, calling, justification, and glorification form one sequence of divine acts with no human co-agent named as a link; glorification is stated in the aorist, as accomplished.
Basis: study 05, finding 7. Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH. Serves: Parts II, VIII.

### Part III: Creation. Very good, upright and mutable

**III.1 (Tov meod).** God's verdict on the finished creation is comprehensive approval: everything made was tov, fitting and excellent of its kind, and the whole was tov meod.
Basis: study 01, findings 1, 2, 3. Tag: Explicit text and Lexical, HIGH. Serves: Parts III, V.

**III.2 (The lexical limit).** The goodness vocabulary of Genesis 1 does not lexically encode incorruptible, immutable, or consummated; the available wholeness vocabulary (tamim, shalem) is not used of creation in Genesis 1-2, and shalem in all its uses describes completion reached, not a beginning.
Basis: study 01, findings 2, 4. Tag: Lexical and Lexical distribution, HIGH. Serves: Parts III, VIII.

**III.3 (The two states).** Creation's first state is very good, upright, and mutable; its final state is confirmed and incorruptible; the two are distinct states of created goodness, and the second exceeds the first in security and glory.
Basis: study 01, finding 5; study 11, finding 5. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM from study 01's side; MEDIUM-HIGH as the joint assessment with study 11. Serves: Parts III, IV, VIII.

**III.4 (Eden not defective).** Nothing in the text or the lexicon supports calling the first state defective.
Basis: study 01, finding 6. Tag: Explicit text with inference, HIGH. Serves: Parts III, V.

**III.5 (Adam's state: posse peccare et posse non peccare).** Adam before the fall was upright, with mind and will in highest rectitude and a will pliable in either direction; he received an aid of the "without which" kind only (adjutorium sine quo non), which he could forsake, and he had not received constancy to persevere. He might have stood; he fell by his own will.
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, secs. 1.1, 3.7 (Augustine, De correptione et gratia 12.33, Latin and Wallis, direct; Calvin, Institutes 1.15.8, Beveridge, direct). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH as a report of the Augustinian and Calvinian loci. Serves: Parts III, IV.

### Part IV: Fall and sin

**IV.1 (The sin-root claim, JD-adopted qualified form).** Sin's root is the self closed in on itself: made for triune loving communion, the fallen self takes itself, rather than God and neighbor, as its governing center. The closure is a condition inherited before it is chosen, and it is the orientation from which particular sins proceed, in distinguishable modes: grasping, defensive, inert, and arbitral. The claim is universal at the level of root and orientation, and deliberately non-committal at the level of individual motive. Qualifications carried verbatim in substance: Q1, the root is condition and orientation, not per-act motive, and guilt is not motive-indexed (Leviticus 4-5; Numbers 15); Q2, "closed" is the load-bearing term, and "interest" expressly includes the negative preference for remaining undisturbed; Q3, self-preservation is a created good and is sin only when absolutized over faithfulness (Proverbs 29:25); Q4, where Scripture does not disclose a motive, none may be asserted (2 Samuel 6).
Basis: research note on sin-root counterexamples, secs. 5, 7; adopted by JD on 2026-11-01 as the governing form for Part IV. Tag: Theological synthesis; the note's class verdicts are HIGH (ignorance, fear), MEDIUM (omission, misguided altruism), and the qualified claim as a whole is carried at MEDIUM-HIGH. Serves: Parts IV, IX.

**IV.2 (Fallen man's state).** Fallen man is in the condition the tradition tags non posse non peccare: the servant of sin is free to sin and will not be free to do right until, being freed from sin, he becomes the servant of righteousness. The loss is by man's own fault and is not from his creation.
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, secs. 1.2, 3.6 (Enchiridion ch. 30 and De correptione et gratia ch. 12, direct; Calvin, Institutes 3.23.8). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH as a report of the loci. Note: the tag itself is the tradition's label for Augustine's prose; it was not found verbatim in any Augustinian text opened, and the treatise must not print it as Augustine's own words. Serves: Part IV.

**IV.3 (The evil will precedes the act).** Pride, the craving for undue exaltation and self-sufficiency, is the beginning of sin; the evil will precedes the evil act, in man and in the devil, whose fall also began in pride.
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, sec. 2.2 and the City of God XI.15 locus (City of God XIV.13, Dods, direct). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH as a report of the loci. Serves: Part IV.

**IV.4 (The grace state).** Under grace the saints receive not a leavable aid but perseverance itself bestowed, an efficacious help by which they cannot help persevering; yet in this life they still fight sin, and no saint in via is in fact sinless (Augustine's sole exception, the Virgin Mary by a singular grace, is his judgment and is flagged as such, not adopted as an axiom).
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, sec. 1.3 (De correptione et gratia ch. 12, direct; De natura et gratia 36.42, secondary). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH as a report of the loci. Serves: Parts IV, VIII.

**IV.5 (Regret grounded in the verdict on evil).** God's regret and grief in Genesis 6:5-7 are grounded in his moral verdict on what humanity had become, and the regret itself is the stated ground of a purposeful act of judgment; regret and decree are not set against each other in the text.
Basis: study 02, findings 1, 5. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH. Serves: Parts IV, V.

### Part V: The decree and the problem of evil

**V.1 (Asymmetrical intention).** In one and the same historical complex, human agents may devise evil against their brother while God intends the deed itself for good; God's intention, as the grammar builds it, has the deed as its object and good as its end, never the evil as evil, and God is nowhere made the deviser of the evil.
Basis: study 03, findings 1, 2, 3. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH (findings 1-2 are Explicit text, HIGH). Serves: Parts V, II.

**V.2 (No bare permission).** The distinction between doing and permitting, used to insulate God from the government of wicked acts, is an invented evasion: men do nothing save at the secret instigation of God, and Satan and the wicked are under his hand and authority, directed to the ends he pleases, while he continues free from every taint. The treatise's operative distinction is between God's one will as secret counsel and as revealed precept (axiom I.3), not between doing and bare permitting.
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, secs. 3.1, 3.2, 3.4 (Calvin, Institutes 1.18.1-2, 4, Beveridge, direct). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH within the treatise's confessional frame. Serves: Part V.

**V.3 (Ordained, and by his own fault).** Man falls according as God's providence ordains, but he falls by his own fault; in the one act, the reasons for which God and man act are different, and the wicked are not excused as though obeying a precept they of set purpose violate.
Basis: research note on Augustine and Calvin, secs. 3.4, 3.6 (Calvin, Institutes 3.23.8; 1.18.4). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH as a report of the loci. Serves: Parts V, IV.

**V.4 (The gratuitousness discipline).** The claim that no suffering is gratuitous in the strong universal sense is a synthesis resting on the wider theodicy premises (WP T1-T3), not on Romans 8:28 alone, and is carried at MEDIUM; Romans 8:28 itself warrants RP for the called (axiom II.3) and expressly does not assert event-by-event indispensability (axiom II.4).
Basis: study 05, finding 6. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM. Serves: Part V.

**V.5 (The felix culpa boundary).** The Fall is regrettable, and necessary relative to God's actual redemptive decree; it is not metaphysically necessary, and no claim is made that the consummation required the Fall as its precondition.
Basis: working paper thesis as preserved in the corpus (WP sec. 2A); study 11, finding 4(ii); axiom III.5. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM; the Revelation silence is Explicit absence, HIGH. Serves: Parts V, VIII. Audited at item (iii) below.

**V.6 (The guardrails).** The epistemic boundary assigns responsibility rather than removing it: the revealed domain is entrusted precisely in order to do, and an appeal to the hidden things that excused known injustice would contradict the boundary's purpose. The boundary likewise never silences lament; that guardrail is carried on the Psalms of lament and Job, not on Deuteronomy 29:29.
Basis: study 04, finding 7. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH (the action half); Theological synthesis, MEDIUM (the lament half, on grounds outside the verse). Serves: Parts V, IX.

### Part VI: Law and accommodation

**VI.1 (The Noahic frame, repaired form).** The Noahic covenant commits God not to destroy all flesh again by flood, despite the persistence of human evil which God himself acknowledges (Genesis 8:21); it rules out universal destruction as the ordinary instrument of moral government in this age, and it positively institutes the preservation of human life and order, including delegated human accountability for bloodshed (Genesis 9:5-6). It does not institute slavery, and it does not foreclose categorical prohibition, local or partial judgment, or differentiated covenant administration.
Basis: research note on the Noahic binary, Step 3 (verdict (b) and repair formulation). Tag: Theological synthesis on textual analysis rated HIGH by the note. Status: drafted on the repair; JD's adoption of the repair is a pending decision (section 4). Serves: Parts VI, V.

**VI.2 (Prohibition and enforcement are separable).** A thing may be categorically prohibited while its enforcement is delegated to human agents, scoped to a covenant people, graded, or delayed; prohibition and immediate comprehensive enforcement are distinct variables, as Genesis 9:6 itself shows.
Basis: research note on the Noahic binary, Steps 2-3. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH. Serves: Part VI.

**VI.3 (The Leviticus 25 dependency).** The classification of Leviticus 25:44-46 as accommodation must rest on text-level criteria internal to scrutiny item 4 (the tension with Leviticus 19:34; the prophetic elevation of liberation, Isaiah 58:6-7 and Jeremiah 34:8-22; the trajectory toward abolition), and must not borrow force from the Noahic claim, repaired or otherwise.
Basis: research note on the Noahic binary, interaction section. Tag: methodological axiom; the dependency is rated HIGH by the note, the division of labor MEDIUM. Status: the item 4 criteria themselves are not yet built (section 4). Serves: Part VI.

### Part VII: Christology

**VII.1 (Two natural wills: the conciliar confession).** Christ has two natural wills and two natural operations, one divine and one human, indivisibly, inconvertibly, inseparably, inconfusedly; the two wills are not contrary, and the human will follows the divine, not resisting or reluctant but subject to it, deified yet not suppressed.
Basis: research note on the two wills, sec. 4, Tier A (Constantinople III, Definition of Faith, Session XVIII, read in the Schaff NPNF2 vol. 14 translation). Tag: conciliar dogma, carried in the treatise's tagging scheme as Theological synthesis, HIGH. Precision: as the council deploys them, John 6:38 stands in the Definition itself; Luke 22:42 enters through Agatho's letter and its Athanasian florilegium. Serves: Part VII.

**VII.2 (The distinction is explicit in the control texts).** One speaker, one "I," predicates two referentially distinct wills in a single sentence (John 6:38), and the Gethsemane contrast "not my will, but yours" is intelligible only if "my will" names something genuinely his and genuinely distinguishable (Luke 22:42).
Basis: study 08, finding 1; study 07, finding 1. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH. Serves: Part VII.

**VII.3 (Recoil and alignment).** Christ's recoil is from the cup, the wrath-bearing passion, and is sinless: natural human aversion to suffering and death presented without any mark of fault. His alignment with the Father's will is free and unforced, the human will's own act; John supplies the constancy pole (recoil real at 12:27 and 13:21, alignment unbroken), Luke the petitionary tension, and Part VII must not import either Gospel's form into the other.
Basis: study 07, findings 2, 3; study 08, finding 2. Tag: Explicit text and Necessary inference, HIGH; the distribution of the two Gospels into a constancy pole (John) and a petitionary-tension pole (Luke) is study 08, finding 2's framing, tagged there Theological synthesis, MEDIUM. Serves: Part VII.

**VII.4 (No gnomic will).** Christ possesses the natural human will and does not possess a gnomic will: gnome is a deliberative mode of using the natural will, formed under ignorance and hesitation in fallen hypostases, not a faculty (logos) of nature.
Basis: research note on the two wills, sec. 4, Tier C2 (Maximus, Opusculum 3 and the Disputation with Pyrrhus as cited in the scholarship the note consulted). Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM. Limit: Maximus was reached at excerpt level only; see section 4. Serves: Part VII.

**VII.5 (Tempted in every respect, sin excluded throughout).** Christ has been tempted in every respect as we are, kata panta distributing over kinds, not individual episodes; and choris hamartias excludes sin from the comparison, from the process of his being tempted, and from its outcome, all at once, so that his temptations never came as solicitation from a sinful desire within. His sympathy is grounded in undergone and completed experience, nowhere in a capacity to fall.
Basis: study 09, findings 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH (findings 1-3 rest on Explicit text, HIGH). Serves: Part VII.

**VII.6 (Impeccability).** Christ could not sin (non posse peccare). As an inference from Hebrews 4:15 alone the claim stands at MEDIUM: the verse confesses the fact of sinlessness and contains no modal term. As the treatise constructs it, on the full premise set (the fact of 4:15 restated as an abiding state at 7:26-28; the same epistle's unchanging Son, 1:12 and 13:8; James 1:13, God untemptable by evil; 1 John 3:5, in him there is no sin; 2 Corinthians 5:21; John 8:46; completed by the one-person premise, that the willing and acting subject is the one divine Son and the works of each nature are the works of the whole person, WLC 40), it is good and necessary consequence at full strength and stands at HIGH as theological synthesis. It is not a conciliar definition and not a confessional article, and it is contested within the tradition.
Basis: study 09, findings 6, 7, 8; research note on the two wills, sec. 4, Tiers A4, B4, C1. Tag: Theological synthesis, dual-basis as stated. The printed calibration for Part VII is proposed and defended at audit item (i). Serves: Part VII.

**VII.7 (The Westminster precision).** The Westminster standards confess two whole, perfect, and entire natures inseparably joined in one person, the human nature complete with all essential properties and common infirmities, yet without sin, each nature doing what is proper to itself and its proper works relied on as the works of the whole person. They formulate neither two wills nor impeccability; the treatise claims consistency and consequence from them for both doctrines, never confessional formulation.
Basis: research note on the two wills, sec. 4, Tier B (WCF 8.2, 8.7; WLC 36-40, read directly). Tag: Explicit text as a report of the standards, HIGH. Serves: Parts VII, Prolegomena.

**VII.8 (Obedience as execution of the decree).** The Father's will that the Son came down to do is specified in John 6:39-40 as a definite saving purpose: to lose nothing of all the Father has given him, to raise it at the last day, and to give eternal life to everyone who looks on the Son and believes. The Son's obedience is therefore the execution of the saving decree, and John 6:38 serves Christology and soteriology together.
Basis: study 08, findings 3, 4. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH (the content claim); Theological synthesis, MEDIUM (the programmatic instruction). Serves: Parts VII, II.

### Part VIII: Consummation. Perfected loving communion

**VIII.1 (The negation chain).** The consummated state is described by absolute negations: death, mourning, crying, and pain shall be no more; every cursed thing (pan katathema) shall be no more; night shall be no more; the sea is no more; and nothing common (koinon) shall by any means enter the city.
Basis: study 11, finding 1. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH. Serves: Part VIII.

**VIII.2 (Permanence of the state).** The state described excludes every consequence of the fall, every agent of corruption (kept outside, 21:27; 22:15; the second death, 21:8), and every occasion of a renewed fall, and it measures the inhabitants' reign by the maximum duration (eis tous aionas ton aionon, 22:5) under the immediate presence of God and the Lamb. A state so defined has no fall left to undergo.
Basis: study 11, finding 2. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH. Serves: Part VIII.

**VIII.3 (The confirmation label).** The saints in glory are confirmed in righteousness, non posse peccare: the last liberty is much greater than the first, not to be able to sin, not to be able to die, not to be able to forsake the good. The label joins Revelation 21-22's exclusions to the Augustinian four-state scheme and to the canon's account of the saints' perseverance, and it describes the inhabitants' interior condition, about which Revelation 21-22 speaks only through the city's boundaries and its occupants' service and reign.
Basis: study 11, finding 2; research note on Augustine and Calvin, sec. 1.4 (De correptione et gratia 12.33; Enchiridion 104-105, direct). Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH. City of God XXII.30 was not opened and is not cited. Serves: Parts VIII, IV.

**VIII.4 (Continuity and discontinuity).** The consummation is discontinuity of the order and continuity of the totality: the first heaven and earth pass away, and God's own summary is "I make all things new" (kaina poio panta), with all things as the object made new; the city descends, Eden's river and tree reappear within it, and the nations and kings of the earth bring their glory in.
Basis: study 11, finding 3. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH, on grammatical and lexical observations rated HIGH. Serves: Part VIII.

**VIII.5 (The consummated vocation).** The inhabitants are God's servants (douloi) who render him priestly service (latreia), see his face, and reign forever and ever: being God's own and exercising dominion coincide permanently, and rest in the consummation is not inactivity nor rule autonomy.
Basis: study 11, finding 6. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH (the simultaneity of service and reign); Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH (the communion framing). Serves: Parts VIII, IX, I.

**VIII.6 (Entrance by gift).** Entrance to the consummation is by inscription in the Lamb's book of life, the sole stated ground of entry, and the right (exousia) to the tree of life belongs to those who wash their robes: the state is populated by gift and cleansing, not by attainment.
Basis: study 11, finding 7. Tag: Explicit text, HIGH, subject to the study's textual note on 22:14 (the critical reading plynontes against the Received Text's poiountes). Serves: Part VIII.

**VIII.7 (Security and the mechanism discipline).** The consummation's security rests on the decree chain of axiom II.8: glorification is a link in God's act, not a contingency. Revelation 21-22 supplies no mechanism of confirmation; any mechanism argument (the saints' transformed wills under God's preserving presence, argued from the wider canon and from axioms VII.1-VII.3) is synthesis and must be presented as such.
Basis: study 05, finding 7; study 11, finding 4(iii). Tag: Theological synthesis, HIGH (the security direction); Explicit absence, HIGH (the mechanism silence). Serves: Part VIII.

### Part IX: Ethics and apologetic deployment

**IX.1 (The archetype applied).** The moral order is read from its archetype (axiom I.2): good is conformity to triune loving communion, and sin is its privation and opposite, the closed self of axiom IV.1, in ethics as in dogmatics; no second, autonomous moral standard is introduced at the point of deployment.
Basis: axioms I.2, IV.1, VIII.5. Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH (inheriting the tags of its premises). Serves: Part IX.

**IX.2 (The Euthyphro resolution).** The good is neither a standard external to God, which he conforms to, nor an arbitrary product of bare will: God's will is itself the highest rule of perfection, the law of all laws, and it is the will of the triune communion whose character is love (axiom I.2), so that what God wills is good because of who God is.
Basis: WP sec. 1 (candidate contribution); research note on Augustine and Calvin, sec. 3.6 (Calvin, Institutes 3.23.2, Battles as quoted, secondary). Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM. Serves: Parts IX, X, I.

**IX.3 (Accommodation as fidelity).** Where God regulates a fallen structure rather than abolishing it at once, within the Noahic constraint of axiom VI.1 and under stated principles (Leviticus 19:18, 34) with judgment applied discriminately, the accommodation is covenantal fidelity and staged administration, not moral compromise or endorsement.
Basis: research note on the Noahic binary, Step 3 (what the repair preserves). Tag: Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH. Status: inherits the pending adoption at axiom VI.1. Serves: Parts IX, VI.

### Part X: Objections register

**X.1 (The level rule).** An objection is answered at its own level (text, inference, philosophy), and no premise may change levels in transit: in particular, "temptation implies the possibility of sin" (Hodge) is a philosophical premise, not an exegetical finding; peirazo's usage does not carry it, and Hebrews 4:15's sympathy logic does not need it.
Basis: study 09, findings 3, 9, and sec. 5.1. Tag: Assessment, HIGH. Serves: Parts X, VII.

**X.2 (Dissent recorded, not suppressed).** Where a theologian of weight inside the treatise's own tradition dissents from a synthesis the treatise adopts, the dissent is recorded in the text and the claim is printed at its contested calibration, not at the calibration of an uncontested claim.
Basis: research note on the two wills, sec. 4, Tier C (the standing dissent of Hodge as itself sufficient reason for care in tagging); study 09, sec. 5.1. Tag: methodological axiom. Serves: Part X, and the tagging of axiom VII.6.

Basis: study 04, findings 2, 5, and sec. 5, reading 5. Tag: Necessary inference, HIGH. Serves: Parts X, V.

## 3. Consistency audit

### Required items

**(i) Impeccability calibration: two-wills note (MEDIUM) against study 09, finding 8 (split MEDIUM / HIGH).**
The two calibrations measure different things, and the audit finds them compatible once that is seen. The two-wills note (sec. 4, Tier C1) tags the tradition-status of the doctrine: impeccability is neither defined by a council (Tier A4: sinlessness is confessed as fact, no modal inability is defined) nor formulated by the Westminster standards (Tier B4), and a theologian of Hodge's weight dissents from it inside the tradition; on that measure its tag is MEDIUM, and the note states what would raise it, namely a tradition-wide demonstration by good and necessary consequence together with an answer to Hodge's definition of temptation that commands the field. Study 09, finding 8, tags the strength of the inference on two specified bases: from Hebrews 4:15 alone, MEDIUM (finding 6: the verse contains no modal term); on the full premise set as the treatise actually constructs it, HIGH, as good and necessary consequence at full strength.
PROPOSED PRINTED CALIBRATION FOR PART VII (one calibration, printed wherever the doctrine is stated): "Impeccability is theological synthesis. From Hebrews 4:15 alone it stands at MEDIUM. On the treatise's full premise set (Hebrews 4:15 with 7:26-28, 1:12, 13:8; James 1:13; 1 John 3:5; 2 Corinthians 5:21; John 8:46; and the one-person premise of WLC 40) it stands at HIGH. It is not a conciliar definition and not a confessional article, and it is contested within the Reformed tradition (Hodge)."
Reasoning. Printing only MEDIUM would understate the inference the treatise actually makes and uses: the treatise never argues impeccability from one verse, and study 09 shows the premise set yields it by good and necessary consequence, which is the treatise's own standard of proof (PRO.3). Printing only HIGH, unqualified, would overstate the doctrine's dogmatic rank and would violate axiom X.2, since the conciliar and confessional silence and Hodge's dissent are facts of the record. The distinction drawn in this item between the doctrine's tradition-status and the strength of the treatise's inference on its stated premise set is the audit's reconciliation, not the two-wills note's own statement: the note grounds its MEDIUM tag in the claim being an inference neither defined nor uncontested, and states that Hodge's standing dissent is itself sufficient reason to keep the tag at MEDIUM, without distinguishing dogmatic rank from inference strength. The printed calibration above is adopted with that difference from the note recorded; it preserves the note's substance by printing the contested qualifier wherever the HIGH synthesis tag is used, and the note's raise-condition still governs any future claim that the doctrine itself stands above MEDIUM in the tradition. Study 09, sec. 5, supplies the treatise's answer to Hodge at the exegetical level (his premise is neither in the lexicon nor in the verse, finding 9), while the philosophical form of his premise remains open debate (axiom X.1), which is exactly why the contested qualifier stays in print.
Disposition: CONSISTENT WITH STATED DISCIPLINE, the discipline being axiom VII.6's dual-basis tag and the printed calibration above.

**(ii) The saints' confirmation label (study 11 reserves non posse peccare at MEDIUM-HIGH synthesis).**
The axiom set carries the label at axiom VIII.3, tagged Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH, with its joining premises named in the axiom itself (Revelation's exclusions, joined to the Augustinian state (d) loci and to the decree chain). The set holds a strict separation between VIII.2 (the state's permanence, Necessary inference, HIGH) and VIII.3 (the label and its application to the saints' wills, synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH): no Part VIII argument may borrow VIII.2's HIGH rating for VIII.3's claim, and axiom VIII.7 bars presenting any mechanism of confirmation as Revelation's teaching. The Augustinian support for the label currently rests on De correptione et gratia 12.33 and Enchiridion 104-105 only; City of God XXII.30 is unopened and uncited (section 4).
Disposition: CONSISTENT WITH STATED DISCIPLINE.

**(iii) The felix culpa boundary: decree-relative necessity (working paper) against study 11's silence finding.**
The joint position, printed at axiom V.5, has three members and all three are needed. (a) The fall actually occurred under the decree, and relative to the redemptive decree God actually made, the fall is presupposed by the redemption decreed: necessity relative to the decree. (b) The fall is not metaphysically necessary: Adam stood in posse peccare et posse non peccare and might have stood (axiom III.5; Calvin, Institutes 1.15.8), so the necessity is not located in Adam's constitution or in God's nature. (c) No text in the anchor set, and Revelation 21-22 expressly, claims that the consummation's glory required the fall as its precondition (study 11, finding 4(ii): the chapters narrate the state that in fact follows redemption and nowhere say the river, the tree, the throne, or the reign required sin); strong felix culpa is therefore not a treatise claim and may not be cited from Revelation 21-22. The working paper's formula, "regrettable but necessary relative to God's actual redemptive decree," is retained with "relative to" load-bearing, and axiom II.4 (no event-by-event indispensability) bars sliding from (a) to the claim that each particular evil was instrumentally necessary.
Disposition: CONSISTENT WITH STATED DISCIPLINE.

**(iv) Ephesians 1:11 (domain, not mode) against the Romans 8:28 governing premise.**
The two texts divide the labor, and each study polices the other's use. Ephesians 1:11 supplies RP's agency and domain: who works, and over what scope (axiom II.1), and study 06, finding 3, confirms it as the corroborant for exactly that component, since Romans 8:28's syntax leaves the agent distributed among construals. Romans 8:28 supplies what Ephesians 1:11 does not: the beneficiaries and the telos (axiom II.3), and study 05, finding 5, shows RP survives the variant on either text's help. Neither supplies the mode of the working, and the set bars the two standing misuses: citing Ephesians 1:11 for mode or reprobation (axiom II.2), and quoting Romans 8:28 as though it alone proved universal no-gratuitousness (axioms II.4, V.4).
Disposition: CONSISTENT.

**(v) Deuteronomy 29:29's epistemic rule: extension, not statement.**
The verse enters the axiom set only as axiom PRO.1 (the boundary of belonging, Explicit text, HIGH) together with its contextual referents (study 04, finding 3: hidden covenant treachery and its judgment on the hidden side, the law on the revealed side). The extension to God's unrevealed counsel and future dealings is legitimate (study 04, finding 4: it begins inside the chapter, at 29:22-28, MEDIUM-HIGH) but is everywhere cited as extension. The epistemic rule of axiom PRO.2 is printed as what study 04, finding 6, shows it to be: a valid consequence of the verse's structure, tagged Theological synthesis, MEDIUM, never as a statement of the verse. Part V citation discipline: the verse may not be cited as addressing any particular evil (finding 5), and the no-silencing-lament guardrail is carried on the Psalms and Job, not on this verse (finding 7; axiom V.6). Disposition: CONSISTENT WITH STATED DISCIPLINE.

**(vi) The pactum (Theological synthesis, MEDIUM) alongside Part I and II claims about the eternal triune life.**
Scrutiny item 5's distinction is kept explicit by a three-level separation that the set never merges. Level 1: the eternal triune life itself (axiom I.2), what God is eternally; it is not a transaction and has no parties, conditions, or promises. Level 2: the eternal decree or counsel (axioms II.1, II.8), God's one determination. Level 3: the pactum (axiom II.6), the covenantal form in which the tradition states the decree toward the Mediator, a construction by collation. Any sentence that moves from Level 1 to Level 3 without passing through Level 2 and the collation commits the level error this item exists to prevent. Guards carried in the set: one will appropriated distinctly, never two wills bargaining (axioms II.6, I.3); the Spirit named explicitly (axiom II.6); Westminster never cited as confessing the pactum, exactly parallel to axiom VII.7's precision for two wills (pactum note, sec. 4); Zechariah 6:13 never cited as a proof text, and Hebrews 13:20 carried as chain member, not source, with its "eternal" lexically endless rather than beginningless (axioms II.6, II.7). The MEDIUM tag attaches to Level 3's scriptural explicitness only; it does not weaken Level 1 or Level 2 claims, which rest on their own bases.
Disposition: CONSISTENT WITH STATED DISCIPLINE.

**(vii) The sin-root claim: JD-adopted qualified form against unqualified corpus statements.**
Within the axiom set there is no tension: only the qualified form is axiomatized (axiom IV.1), and any draft in the unqualified universal-motive form is barred by the note's evidence (Leviticus 4-5; Numbers 15). At the level of the corpus, a tension stands: earlier corpus formulations of the root claim (notably the compact statement in the triune-loving-communion systematic theology, where the Fall is stated as closed self-interest without the Q1-Q4 qualifications on its face) can be read in the unqualified sense the stress test refuted. The resolution path has been adopted by JD (the qualified formulation, 2026-11-01); what remains is execution, locus by locus, in Part IV drafting, which this audit cannot perform from the sources in hand.
Disposition: TENSION REQUIRING RESOLUTION at the corpus level, with the resolution path adopted and stated; CONSISTENT within the axiom set.
