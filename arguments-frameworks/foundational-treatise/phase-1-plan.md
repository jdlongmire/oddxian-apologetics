## Order

Systematic means canonical order through the anchor texts, one study per text, each study closing the gaps the existing corpus has already named for that text before the next study begins. The working paper (draft PR #5) is the starting coverage, not a blank page: its source verification register (section 6.21) and research plan (section 7) define what is established, what is partial, and what is open for each text.

## Per-unit method

Each exegetical study contains, in this order:

1. **Text and translation.** Primary text, translation comparison where the argument turns on a rendering, textual-critical notes where a variant matters.
2. **Context.** Immediate literary context and canonical placement; no claim from an isolated clause.
3. **Word studies.** Original-language lexical work from the lexicon (BDB, and HALOT where available), with the lexicon's mediation labeled. Semantic claims rest on usage in context, not etymology.
4. **Competing readings.** The serious alternative readings, stated fairly, with the reasons the study adopts or declines each.
5. **Findings with claim tags.** Every finding tagged Explicit text / Necessary inference / Theological synthesis / Philosophical conjecture, with confidence HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / UNCERTAIN, per the work package quality gates.
6. **Gap statement.** What remains open after the study, stated as precisely as the findings.

## Rules

- A study is "complete (working draft)" when its named gaps are closed or carried forward as explicitly open items. Completion is not a claim of critical-edition verification where only translations and lexicons were consulted; mediation is labeled.
- Each unit lands on its own branch as a draft PR for JD review, or is batched with adjacent units where JD directs. No merge without JD's approval.
- Findings feed the Scripture matrix (`scripture-matrix.md`) in the same commit that files the study.
