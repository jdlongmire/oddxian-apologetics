CONFIRMED.

## Study 08: John 6:38

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS.** One factual correction is required (item 2): the transliteration of 6:38 prints the Byzantine preposition where the critical text reads another. Nothing in the findings turns on it; it must be fixed before the study's transliteration is quoted as the critical text.

1. The hina clause structure of 6:38. The critical text, as parsed in the BibleHub display for John 6:38, runs: hoti katabebeka apo tou ouranou ouch hina poio to thelema to emon alla to thelema tou pempsantos me, with poio parsed "Verb - Present Subjunctive Active - 1st Person Singular," emon "Personal/Possessive Pronoun - Accusative Neuter 1st Person Singular," and pempsantos "Verb - Aorist Participle Active - Genitive Masculine Singular." An independent clause analysis (B-Greek forum discussion of the verse) prints the same text and the same three-clause structure, with the second will-phrase elliptical for the repeated hina poio. The study's grammatical description (purpose negation ouch hina; present subjunctive poio; two articular noun phrases, to thelema to emon against to thelema tou pempsantos me; the second member elliptical) is exact. CONFIRMED.
2. CORRECTED. Section 1 transliterates the verse's opening as "katabebeka ek tou ouranou" and states that the working paper's transliteration (with ek) "agrees." At John 6:38 the critical text reads APO tou ouranou: the BibleHub parse gives the preposition as apo (Strong's 575), and the independent interlinear and clause-analysis sources consulted print apo at 6:38. EK tou ouranou is the Byzantine/Textus Receptus reading at this verse (it is the reading behind the KJV's tradition and Young's Literal Translation's "out of the heaven"); within chapter 6 the critical text does read ek at 6:33, 41, 42, 50, 51, and 58, which is presumably how the ek form traveled into the study's 6:38. Correct fact: at 6:38 read "katabebeka apo tou ouranou" for the critical text; ek is a variant (Byzantine/TR). The correction touches the transliteration in section 1 (and the agreement remark about the working paper, which should be qualified), and section 6's statement that the text of 6:38 is stable should except this preposition variant alongside the two it already records. The descent claim, the perfect tense, and both will-phrases are identical on either preposition; no finding is affected.
3. Katabebeka as perfect. BibleHub's parse of John 6:38: katabebeka, "Verb - Perfect Indicative Active - 1st Person Singular" (a second interlinear display tags it V-RIA-1S). The study's claim (perfect tense; the versions' "have come down" against the KJV's "came down"; the crowd quoting the perfect back at 6:42) is exact. CONFIRMED.
