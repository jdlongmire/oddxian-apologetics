**Method:** A decisive sample of each study's factual claims was re-checked against sources consulted independently in this session: BDB as reproduced at bibleapps.com (H2803, full entry read); the NET Bible text and textual notes at classic.net.bible.org and BibleGateway; the StudyLight Greek and Hebrew interlinear displays; parallel translation displays (StudyLight, bible.com, Biblia, BibleGateway); Metzger's Textual Commentary as reproduced at bibliaplus.org and in the PuritanBoard verbatim quotation; the Westminster Confession with proof texts in two independent reproductions (dougwils.com; pilgrim-covenant.com PDF); the puncta extraordinaria literature at thetorah.com and sofer.co.uk; the OpenBible Greek text display (Nestle 1904 and Scrivener's Textus Receptus 1894).

## Study 03: Genesis 50:20

**Verdict: PASS.**

1. BDB's arrangement for man (Qal I). The study's five senses and citations match the entry as reproduced: I.1 think, account (Isaiah 10:7; two accusatives, Isaiah 53:4; accusative plus lamed, Genesis 38:15); I.2 devise, plan, mean (machashevet ra'ah, Ezekiel 38:10; ra'ah, Psalm 35:4, 140:3, Zechariah 7:10, 8:17; mezimmot, Psalm 10:2, 21:12; avon, Micah 2:1, Ezekiel 11:2, Psalm 36:5; ra'ah al, "devise evil against," Genesis 50:20, Jeremiah 48:2, Nahum 1:11; machashavot al, Jeremiah 11:19, 18:18, 49:30, Esther 8:3, 9:25; with infinitive, 1 Samuel 18:25, Nehemiah 6:2, 6); I.3 charge, impute (2 Samuel 19:20); I.4 esteem, value (Isaiah 33:8, 53:3); I.5 invent (Exodus 31:4, Amos 6:5). CONFIRMED (BDB H2803, bibleapps.com).
2. BDB's arrangement for God (Qal II). II.1 think, account one as an enemy (Job 13:24, 19:11, 33:10); II.2 devise, plan, mean; II.3 impute, reckon (Genesis 15:6; Psalm 32:2). CONFIRMED (same entry).
3. The Genesis 50:20 divine construction. BDB II.2 opens: "with accusative + lamed indirect object, letovah, for good, Genesis 50:20," and Genesis 50:20 is the only citation given for that exact construction; the remaining II.2 citations are of other shapes (lamed of person, Psalm 40:18 in BDB's Hebrew numbering; accusative plus al, Micah 2:3, Jeremiah 18:11; toward, Jeremiah 29:11; with el, Jeremiah 49:20, 50:45; with infinitive, Jeremiah 26:3, 36:3, Lamentations 2:8). The study's claim that BDB files the clause under "devise, plan, mean" (II.2), not under "impute, reckon" (II.3), and that the letovah construction is uniquely cited from this verse, is exact. CONFIRMED.
4. The Genesis 15:6 construction contrast. BDB II.3 is defined as "with accusative of thing + lamed person" and cites Genesis 15:6 ("he reckoned it to him as righteousness") and Psalm 32:2. The study's description (accusative plus lamed of the person credited plus a bare predicate noun, against 50:20's accusative plus lamed of destination) matches BDB's own construction distinction. CONFIRMED.
5. The Septuagint wording. The printed LXX text in the StudyLight Greek interlinear reads: hymeis ebouleusasthe kat' emou eis ponera, ho de theos ebouleusato peri emou eis agatha, hopos an genethe hos semeron, hina diatraphe laos polus. Both clauses use bouleuo in the middle voice (ebouleusasthe, ebouleusato); God's clause has peri emou where the Hebrew has the accusative suffix; the purpose clause ends hina diatraphe laos polus. The study's report, including its caution that the interlinear's parsing column wrongly repeats the second plural form for God's verb while the printed text reads ebouleusato, is accurate in every particular checked. CONFIRMED.
6. Brenton's rendering. "Ye took counsel against me for evil, but God took counsel for me for good, that the matter might be as it is to-day, and much people might be fed" (ebible.org updated Brenton; StudyLight Brenton display). The study's quotation is verbatim for the portion quoted. CONFIRMED.
7. Translation table and the symmetry claim. KJV (ye thought / God meant it unto good), ESV (meant / meant), NASB (meant / meant), CSB (planned / planned), LEB (planned / planned) verified in the StudyLight interlinear and CSB displays; NIV (intended to harm / intended it for good), NET (meant to harm / intended it for a good purpose), NRSV (intended to do harm / intended it for good) verified in the parallel displays at bible.com, Biblia, and BibleGateway. The study's claim that the formal versions preserve the one-verb symmetry, that NET differentiates slightly and expands letovah as "for a good purpose," and that the NCV breaks the symmetry ("You meant to hurt me, but God turned your evil into good," bible.com NCV) is correct on every row checked. CONFIRMED.
8. Masoretic stability for the verse. BHS, the Aleppo Codex, and the Westminster Leningrad Codex as printed in the interlinear display agree word for word, including both chashav forms and the suffix on chashavah. CONFIRMED for the witnesses named; the study states its own collation limit (Samaritan Pentateuch and Targumim not collated), which stands as an open item, not an error.

No corrections. The study's findings 1-6 were not relitigated; their factual substructure (items 1-8) is sound.

## Study 04: Deuteronomy 29:29

**Verdict: PASS.**

1. Verse numbering. The NET study note on Deuteronomy 29:1 states verbatim: "Beginning with 29:1, the verse numbers through 29:29 in the English Bible differ from the verse numbers in the Hebrew text (BHS), with 29:1 ET = 28:69 HT, 29:2 ET = 29:1 HT ... through 29:29 ET = 29:28 HT" (BibleGateway NET display; the same note at bible-history.com). The study's claim (English 29:29 = Hebrew 29:28; numbers differ by one from 29:1 to 29:29; NET textual note on the chapter) is exact, including the attribution. CONFIRMED.
2. Puncta extraordinaria: placement and count. Thetorah.com: "the verse has eleven dotted letters"; dots stand over lanu ulevaneinu ("to us and to our children") and over the ayin of ad; the Tosafot gloss explains the ayin as dotted "in order that eleven letters will be dotted." Sofer.co.uk: eleven dots over the three words, one over each letter except the final dalet of ad; also ten dotted passages in the Torah and fifteen dotted words in the Tanakh, matching the study's parenthetical counts. The study's claim (dots over each letter of lanu ulevaneinu plus the ayin of ad, eleven letters) is exact. CONFIRMED.
3. The Sanhedrin 43b dispute. As quoted at thetorah.com from b. Sanhedrin 43b: Rabbi Judah (Yehuda): God "did not punish [them] for violations of hidden things until the Israelites crossed the Jordan." Rabbi Nehemiah: "Does [God] ever punish for hidden things? Doesn't it say 'forever'? Rather, just as he doesn't punish for hidden things ever, so too he didn't punish for public punishable offenses until the Israelites crossed the Jordan." The study's characterization (Yehuda: no punishment for hidden sins until the crossing; Nehemiah: hidden things are never punished, and collective punishment even for revealed things began only after the crossing, with mutual responsibility) matches the talmudic text as quoted. CONFIRMED.
4. Westminster Confession 3.8 proof texts. Two independent reproductions of the Confession with its proof apparatus (dougwils.com inline citations; pilgrim-covenant.com footnoted edition) give for the clause "is to be handled with special prudence and care" the proof texts Romans 9:20, Romans 11:33, and Deuteronomy 29:29, in that order; 2 Peter 1:10 attaches to the following assurance clause. The study's claim that WCF 3.8 cites Deuteronomy 29:29 among its proof texts, with Romans 9:20 and 11:33, for the prudence-and-care direction, is exact. CONFIRMED. The study's further remark that the confessional use governs manner rather than supplying matter is its own stated observation, not a factual claim about the apparatus, and is not disputed here.
5. Neither participle carries an object. In the Masoretic text as printed (thetorah.com, Aleppo highlighting; the verse is Hebrew 29:28), hannistarot is followed by laYHWH Eloheinu and hanniglot by lanu ulevaneinu ad-olam, with the verse closing in the purpose infinitive laasot et-kol-divrei hattorah hazzot. Neither substantivized participle takes an object, a genitive, or a qualifying complement; the lamed phrases assign belonging, and the only infinitive is the closing purpose clause. The study's grammatical claim, and its use of it (content of the domains is unspecified by the wording), is correct. CONFIRMED. The supporting note that Psalm 19:12 in English numbering (19:13 in Hebrew) uses min-nistarot for hidden faults is also correct as stated.
6. Translation table (section 1 of the study). UNVERIFIED in detail. The five versions' wordings were not re-displayed line by line in this session. No discrepancy is suspected: the table's structural claim (antithesis, belonging, purpose clause in all five) is consistent with the NET note on the verse (the "sons" of the Hebrew, against "children" in KJV, NIV, and NRSV) and with the verse text consulted for items 1, 2, and 5. The study's argument does not rest on any single table wording. No action required; recorded for completeness.

No corrections. Findings 1-8 rest on items 1-5, all confirmed.

## Study 05: Romans 8:28-30

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS.** Two factual corrections are required (items 3 and 6). Neither overturns a finding; both sit in the study's supporting apparatus (sections 6 and 3.1) and must be fixed before those sections are cited downstream.

1. The shorter text in Nestle 1904 and in Scrivener's Textus Receptus 1894. The OpenBible display prints both texts of Romans 8:28 identically, without ho theos (shown only parenthesized as the variant), and tags panta Adj-ANP (form ambiguous between nominative and accusative) and synergei V-PIA-3S. The study's claims (both texts agree on the shorter reading; the KJV, translated from the Textus Receptus, therefore takes panta as subject; the morphology notes) are correct. CONFIRMED.
2. The NET textual note: quotations. The note at classic.net.bible.org reads, in the parts the study quotes: "the longer reading is evidently motivated by a need for clarification"; "the subject is embedded in the verb and 'God' is clearly implied (as in v. 29)"; and it closes with the Cranfield sentence, "What is expressed is a truly biblical confidence in the sovereignty of God" (Romans [ICC], 1:427). All three quotations are verbatim. CONFIRMED.
3. The NET textual note: witness lists. CORRECTED. The longer-reading list is as the study gives it: P46, A, B, 81, sa (NET note, classic.net.bible.org). The shorter-reading list in the NET note as displayed reads: Aleph, C, D, F, G, Psi, 33, 1739, 1881, the Byzantine majority, latt, sy, bo. The study's list inserts four further minuscules, 1175, 1241, 1505, and 2464, under the heading "Attestation, as reported in the NET textual note." Those four witnesses do not appear in the NET note consulted. They are of the kind cited in the NA28 apparatus for this unit, but the NA28 apparatus was not consulted in this session (the study itself lists direct NA28 consultation as an open item), so their attestation is not independently confirmed here. Correct fact: the NET note's shorter list is Aleph C D F G Psi 33 1739 1881 Byz latt sy bo, full stop; the four additional minuscules must either be deleted from the NET attribution or re-sourced to a directly consulted NA28 apparatus. Source: NET tc note on Romans 8:28, classic.net.bible.org, read in full in this session.
4. Metzger: the UBS rating. The Textual Commentary entry is headed "ROMANS 8:28 synergei {B}" (bibliaplus.org reproduction of the Commentary), and a second reproduction quotes the entry verbatim: "Although the reading synergei ho theos is both ancient and noteworthy, a majority of the Committee deemed it too narrowly supported to be admitted into the text, particularly in view of the diversified support for the shorter reading... Since synergei may be taken to imply a personal subject, ho theos seems to have been a natural explanatory addition made by an Alexandrian editor" (PuritanBoard thread quoting the Commentary, with Wallace's "scribal tendency toward explicitness" quoted separately, as the study also represents it). The study's claims (rating B for the shorter text; ho theos a "natural explanatory addition made by an Alexandrian editor") are exact. CONFIRMED.
5. Metzger: the longer-reading attestation additions. The Commentary's entry, as reproduced at bibliaplus.org, gives the longer reading as P46 A B 81 copsa (eth) Origengr 2/5, that is, it adds the Ethiopic and Origen in two of five Greek citations to the NET note's list, exactly as the study states. CONFIRMED.
6. Synergeo: the occurrence profile. CORRECTED in one member. The count is right: synergeo (G4903) occurs exactly five times in the New Testament (Mark 16:20; Romans 8:28; 1 Corinthians 16:16; 2 Corinthians 6:1; James 2:22), per the Strong's concordance displays consulted (knowing-jesus.com G4903; paulshandkerchief.com G4903). The subjects in Mark 16:20 (the Lord), 1 Corinthians 16:16 (the substantival participle, designating persons who join the work), and 2 Corinthians 6:1 (Paul and his companions) are personal, and the sense is cooperative in all four remaining occurrences. But the study's sentence, "in every other occurrence its subject is personal," is wrong for James 2:22, where the subject is faith: Gignilliat, "Working Together with Whom?" Biblica 87 (2006) 513 n.11, states expressly, citing Fee, that "in Jas 2,22 synergei takes a non-personal subject but is clearly operating with a cooperative idea, i.e., faith and works." Correct fact: personal subjects in three of the other four occurrences; in James 2:22 the subject is faith (a human faculty, grammatically non-personal) with the cooperative sense intact. The study itself lists "faith working with works (James 2:22)" in the same sentence, so the fix is to the generalization, not to the data. The study tags the weight given the profile as a judgment; that tagging is unaffected, and finding 5 does not rest on this sentence alone. Correction required in section 3.1 before downstream citation.
7. Metzger: the shorter-reading list in full. UNVERIFIED in its enumerated detail. The study states that Metzger's list adds, for the shorter reading, K, P, 614, the Byzantine lectionaries, the Old Latin and Vulgate, the Peshitta and Harklean Syriac, the Armenian, and Clement, Eusebius, Lucifer, Cyril of Jerusalem, Chrysostom, and Augustine, with Origen's remaining citations on the shorter side. The bibliaplus reproduction of the Commentary truncates the entry immediately after "the diversified support for the shorter reading," and the PuritanBoard verbatim quotation elides the same list with an ellipsis, so the enumeration could not be displayed in full from any source retrieved in this session. The list is consistent with the Commentary's known form and nothing retrieved contradicts it; it is recorded as unverified, not as an error. If the coordinator wants it cited downstream, it should be re-checked against the printed Commentary or a complete reproduction.
8. Ephesians 1:11. The Greek text reads kata prothesin tou ta panta energountos kata ten boulen tou thelematos autou (BGT as displayed at preceptaustin.org; parsing at BibleHub: energountos, present participle active, genitive masculine singular, with panta as accusative neuter plural object). The study's claim (Ephesians 1:11 reads tou ta panta energountos, "of him who works all things," with the cognate verb energeo and ta panta as its object) is exact. CONFIRMED.
9. Thayer on synergeo and prothesis (section 3.1 and 3.3 of the study). UNVERIFIED. The study's Thayer material (the classification of Romans 8:28 under "with a dative and eis, to assist, help one for a thing"; the Fritzsche breviloquence note; the prothesis citation lists) is cited from the Blue Letter Bible lexicon pages. That lexicon page returned a server error in this session and, per instruction, was not retried through any route. Nothing retrieved contradicts the Thayer claims, and none of the study's findings rests on the Fritzsche note; the prothesis sense division (divine purpose at Romans 8:28, 9:11, Ephesians 1:11, 3:11, 2 Timothy 1:9; human resolve at Acts 11:23, 27:13, 2 Timothy 3:10) is consistent with the verse texts themselves. Recorded as unverified, no action required before use, provided the mediation label the study already carries ("via Blue Letter Bible") is kept.
