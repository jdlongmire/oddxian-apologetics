## Step 1: What scrutiny item 4 requires, and what the manuscript currently assumes

**The scrutiny statement (verbatim, item 4):** "Treat Leviticus 25:44-46 as a serious countertext and establish independent criteria for accommodation." The corpus-alignment review adds that the manuscript "develops Leviticus 19:18 and 19:34 as moral criteria for interpreting slavery legislation" and "explicitly acknowledges Leviticus 25:44-46 as a difficult text," while its Noahic covenant argument "claims a binary necessity of divine governance, which requires independent logical defense." Two requirements follow. First, criteria: the classification of a Mosaic regulation as accommodation rather than endorsement must rest on criteria established independently, not on the conclusion they are meant to support. Second, independence from the binary: the W2 Noahic note (research-noahic-binary.md) found the binary fails as stated and warned, in its interaction section, that the binary "was doing part of item 4's work by supplying a global reason to classify the slavery regulations as accommodations," and that under the repair "the classification of Leviticus 25:44-46 as accommodation must therefore rest more heavily on item 4's own text-level criteria." This note honors that instruction: the Noahic material appears below only as an age-description, never as a premise in the classification argument. [Explicit text, HIGH]

**The manuscript's classification and its operative criteria.** The manuscript ("Love Your Neighbor as Yourself: Refuting the 'God Endorses Slavery' Error," arguments-frameworks/slavery-argument/biblical_slavery_final_integrated.md, main blob 517f17d7 at retrieval) classifies Leviticus 25:44-46 as accommodation, in its own words: "this represents accommodation at its most challenging"; the permission "represents accommodation to human hardness of heart, constrained by injury protections and the neighbor-love principle that would eventually make it untenable"; and, in its section V return to the text, "This is pedagogical staging within necessary accommodation." Its operative classificatory criteria, extracted from its argument (reconstruction, not quotation), are: (a) tension with the stated principle of sojourner-love (Leviticus 19:34 against 25:44-46); (b) general protections that cross the ethnic line (injury manumission, Exodus 21:26-27; fugitive protection, Deuteronomy 23:15-16); (c) the Matthew 19:8 category, applied by analogy ("Jesus's comment on divorce applies equally here"); (d) prophetic elevation of principle over institution; (e) the Noahic binary as the global "why" (now retired by the Noahic note's verdict); and (f) Paul's citational silence regarding the permission texts. Of these, (a) to (d) and (f) survive the Noahic verdict; (e) does not, and no part of this note's verdict depends on it. [Necessary inference from the manuscript's text, HIGH]

**One manuscript claim flagged for correction in Part VI.** The manuscript argues that the cross-ethnic protections mean foreign servitude cannot be chattel: injury manumission and fugitive protection "would be incomprehensible in a true chattel slavery system." That is stronger than the evidence bears. Protections and chattel status coexist in the ancient Near Eastern material consulted: Hammurabi's laws limit a debtor's household service to three years with release in the fourth (LH 117), yet treat an actual slave given over to forced labor as freely sublet or sold (LH 118) (King translation, consulted via Wikisource and the Fordham sourcebook text; mediation labeled in Sources). Mitigating protections therefore do not by themselves negate a property status; what they can do is stand in tension with the institution's logic and, over the canon's course, subvert it. Part VI should claim the trajectory effect, not the negation. [Necessary inference, MEDIUM-HIGH]

## Step 2: The text and the asymmetry, stated exactly

**The unit.** Leviticus 25:35-55 is one sustained treatment of the impoverished brother and of servitude, framed by the Jubilee legislation of 25:8-34. Its movement: support the poor brother without interest (25:35-38, grounded in the Exodus, v. 38); the brother who sells himself to an Israelite is to be treated "as a hired worker and as a sojourner" (kesakhir ukhetoshav, v. 40), serves only until the Jubilee, and goes out with his children (v. 41), "for they are my servants (avdai), whom I brought out of the land of Egypt; they shall not be sold as slaves are sold" (lo yimmakheru mimkeret eved, v. 42); the Israelite master "shall not rule over him ruthlessly" (lo tirdeh vo bepharekh, v. 43). Then, without transition softening the contrast, verses 44-46: "As for your male and female slaves whom you may have: you may buy male and female slaves from among the nations that are around you. You may also buy from among the strangers who sojourn with you and their clans that are with you, who have been born in your land, and they may be your property (vehayu lakhem la'achuzzah). You may bequeath them to your sons after you to inherit as a possession forever (vehithnachaltem otam levneikhem achareikhem lareshet achuzzah). You may make slaves of them, but over your brothers the people of Israel you shall not rule, one over another ruthlessly." The unit closes (vv. 47-55) with the Israelite sold to a resident foreigner: redeemable at any time, priced as a hired worker, protected from ruthless rule even by the foreign master ("he shall not rule ruthlessly over him in your sight," v. 53), released at the Jubilee regardless, "for it is to me that the people of Israel are servants" (v. 55). [Explicit text, HIGH]

**F1. The operative vocabulary of verses 44-46 is the vocabulary of landed property, and the chapter knows it.** Achuzzah (H272) is, in BDB's classification as reproduced by Blue Letter Bible, "possession, P and late; of landed property," occurring 66 times, overwhelmingly of land held by inheritance (including Leviticus 25 itself at vv. 10, 13, 25, 27, 28, 33, 41, of land and houses). Hitnachaltem is the Hithpael of nachal (H5157), to take or transmit as an inheritance (nachalah). Le'olam (H5769) in verse 46 marks the holding as without term. The same word-pair, achuzzah plus olam, describes God's gift of Canaan to Abraham's seed: "achuzzat olam," an everlasting possession (Genesis 17:8). In verse 23 of this same chapter, perpetuity language is refused for the land itself: the land "shall not be sold in perpetuity (litzmitut), for the land is mine; for you are strangers and sojourners (gerim vetoshavim) with me." A chapter that refuses permanent alienation of land because God owns it, and that grounds Israelite non-enslavability in God's ownership of Israelites by redemption (vv. 42, 55), grants permanent, heritable, property status over foreign persons. The grant is deliberate drafting, not residue. [Explicit text for the vocabulary and the contrasts; Necessary inference for deliberateness, HIGH]

**F2. The asymmetry is threefold and structural.** (1) Term: Israelite service is term-limited twice over (six years, Exodus 21:2 and Deuteronomy 15:12; Jubilee release, Leviticus 25:40-41, whichever the operative code), and even the Israelite sold to a foreigner goes out at the Jubilee (v. 54). Foreign service under verses 44-46 has no term and no release provision of any kind. (2) Status: the Israelite debtor is legally reclassified as a hired worker (sakhir) and sojourner (toshav), "not... serve as a slave" (lo ta'avod vo avodat eved, v. 39); the foreigner is classified as achuzzah, a possession. There is a pointed lexical reversal here: the Israelite debtor receives the toshav label as a protection, while the children of actual toshavim, born in the land, are named as the purchasable class (v. 45). (3) Transmission: the Israelite goes out "he and his children with him" (v. 41), so his children's status is not inherited bondage; the foreign slave is himself inherited, passing to the owner's sons (v. 46), and the purchasable class expressly includes the sojourners' offspring "born in your land" (v. 45), so the status is hereditary on both sides of the transaction. No reading of the chapter dissolves this asymmetry; any honest classification must carry it as a datum. [Explicit text, HIGH]

**F3. The ruthlessness prohibition is drawn at the ethnic line, three times.** Lo tirdeh (bepharekh) appears at verses 43 (Israelite master over Israelite), 46 (the contrast clause), and 53 (foreign master over Israelite, enforceable "in your sight"). Bepharekh is Egypt's word: it describes Pharaoh's oppression of Israel (Exodus 1:13-14, cross-referenced in the ESV apparatus at each Leviticus occurrence). The verse 46 contrast is structured so that the prohibition's stated object is the Israelite brother alone. The greatest codifier of the tradition read the implication plainly: Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Laws of Slaves 9:8, as reproduced in Sommer's study guide (Sources), states "It is permissible to work a Canaanite slave harshly (bepharekh), but although this is the law (din), the quality of lovingkindness (middat hachasidut) and the ways of wisdom are that a person should be compassionate..." Whatever Part VI concludes about classification, it must not claim that Leviticus 25 prohibits ruthless rule over the foreign slave. It does not, in terms. [Explicit text, HIGH]

**F4. Two cross-currents the design reading must also carry.** (a) The foreign slave is inside the covenant household religiously: bought slaves are circumcised (Genesis 17:12-13, in a passage calling the covenant in the flesh "an everlasting covenant," berit olam; Exodus 12:44), after which the bought slave eats the Passover, in a unit that concludes "there shall be one law (torah achat) for the native and for the stranger who sojourns among you" (Exodus 12:49; compare Leviticus 24:22, "the same rule for the sojourner and for the native"), and the slave rests on the Sabbath (Exodus 20:10, as the manuscript notes). (b) The chapter's own Jubilee proclamation is worded without ethnic restriction: "proclaim liberty (deror) throughout the land to all its inhabitants (lekhol yoshveha)" (v. 10), even though the implementing release provisions that follow address Israelites. The deror word is the one Isaiah 61:1 takes up for the eschatological proclamation (the ESV apparatus cross-references Isaiah 61:1 at v. 10), which Jesus adopts as his charter (Luke 4:19). Neither cross-current cancels F1 to F3; both show the text's property grant sits inside a canon that keeps supplying premises the grant does not itself draw. [Explicit text, HIGH for the textual facts; the synthesis weight is assessed in Step 5]

## Step 3: The criteria, derived from the canon and tested against controls

A criterion earns its place here only if it classifies the control cases correctly. Two controls anchor the test. **Endorsement control: marriage.** In Matthew 19:4-6 Jesus grounds marriage in creation ("he who created them from the beginning made them male and female... what therefore God has joined together, let not man separate," citing Genesis 1:27 and 2:24): positively instituted, divine agency named, no fallenness etiology, reaffirmed against the Mosaic regulation. **Accommodation control: divorce.** In the same pericope (Matthew 19:7-8), the Pharisees say Moses "commanded" a certificate; Jesus corrects the verb: "Because of your hardness of heart Moses allowed (epetrepsen) you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so." The New Testament here performs a classification itself, and it is the treatise's charter for the category: a genuine Mosaic permission, really law, that is nevertheless not God's instituted will, identified by an etiology (hardness of heart), a permission-versus-command grammar, and a prior norm ("from the beginning") against which the permission is measured and toward which Jesus re-points. A second accommodation control, Deuteronomy 21:10-14 (the war captive taken as wife), tests the criteria where no dominical saying exists: the law regulates a practice it nowhere commands or praises, hedges it with waiting, mourning, and status protections, and strips the captor of the property consequence (if he dismisses her, "you shall not sell her for money, nor shall you treat her as a slave, since you have humiliated her"). An intra-chapter endorsement control is also available: the Jubilee land law itself (Leviticus 25:10-17, 23) is commanded ("you shall"), grounded in God's own ownership and action, and celebrated in the canon's later use (Isaiah 61:1). [Explicit text, HIGH]

**C1. Etiological marker.** Does the text, or the canon's authoritative interpretation of it, attribute the practice to human hardness or fallenness rather than to divine institution? Derivation: Matthew 19:8 (divorce); the monarchy pattern (1 Samuel 8:7, as the manuscript deploys it). Control test: marriage fails the marker (creational institution instead) -- correct; divorce passes -- correct; captive bride passes only weakly (no etiology is stated; the inference runs from the restraints). Sustained as a discriminator, with the note that it may be supplied canonically rather than locally. [Theological synthesis as a criterion, MEDIUM-HIGH]

**C2. Principle tension.** Does the regulation stand in demonstrable tension with a principle stated in the same legal corpus, which the corpus and its reception treat as weightier than the regulation? Derivation: Leviticus 19:18 as the law's summation (Matthew 22:37-40, Paul in Romans 13:8-10 and Galatians 5:14, all as the manuscript assembles them); Leviticus 19:34, the same Holiness stratum commanding love of the sojourner "as yourself," grounded in Israel's own sojourn ("for you were strangers in the land of Egypt"); Deuteronomy 10:18-19 (God "loves the sojourner... Love the sojourner, therefore"); the one-law texts (Exodus 12:49; Leviticus 24:22); Job 31:13-15, the common-Maker protest from within the canon ("Did not he who made me in the womb make him?"). Control test: the land law shows no such tension (its rationale, divine ownership, is consonant throughout) -- correct; divorce shows maximal tension with Genesis 2:24, which is precisely Jesus' argument -- correct. Sustained. [Necessary inference, HIGH that the tension criterion discriminates; the weight assignment to the principle is Theological synthesis]

**C3. Subversive protections.** Does the surrounding legislation attach to the regulated class protections that contradict the institution's own property logic, protections that would be pointless if the person were straightforwardly a thing? Derivation: injury manumission (Exodus 21:26-27: the slave whose eye or tooth the master destroys "shall go free," stated of "his slave, male or female," without ethnic restriction in terms); fugitive asylum (Deuteronomy 23:15-16: the escaped slave is not to be returned, chooses his own dwelling place, and "you shall not wrong him" -- a standing defeater of the master's property claim at the border of the system); Sabbath rest and covenant-table inclusion (Exodus 20:10; 12:44). Control test, with an honesty check: protections can coexist with property status (Step 1, the Hammurabi datum), and the Covenant Code itself states the property frame without embarrassment ("if the slave survives a day or two, he is not to be avenged, for the slave is his money," kaspo, Exodus 21:21). C3 therefore cannot bear a verdict alone. It discriminates only as tension-evidence: protections that cross the line the institution itself draws (as injury manumission and fugitive asylum cross the ethnic line of Leviticus 25:44-46) show the system's own norms working against the institution's logic. Divorce control: Deuteronomy 24's certificate functions this way (it protects the dismissed wife's remarriage status), weakly. Marriage control: no subversion needed, protections serve the institution. Sustained, as corroborating tension-evidence only. [Necessary inference, MEDIUM]

**C4. Permission grammar.** Is the practice permitted and regulated (concessive, casuistic, "as for... you may") rather than commanded, instituted, or supplied with a rationale that celebrates it? Derivation: Jesus' verb correction at Matthew 19:7-8 (allowed, not commanded); the casuistic form of the slave laws generally. Control test: marriage is instituted with divine agency ("God has joined") -- correct; Jubilee is commanded with rationale -- correct; divorce and captive bride are permissions hedged with conditions -- correct. Sustained. The criterion distinguishes permission from institution; it does not reduce permission to nothing. A permission in Torah is real law and really permitted, which is why the accommodation reading must never be stated as if the text "does not really allow" what it allows. [Explicit text / Necessary inference, HIGH]

**C5. Ungeneralized restrictive rationale.** Where the corpus absolutely protects one class, is the stated ground of protection of a kind the corpus itself generalizes elsewhere, so that the protection's restriction to one class reads as staging rather than as a principled terminus? Derivation: Leviticus 25:42, 55 protects Israelites on a redemption ground ("they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt"); the same Exodus-memory ground is expressly generalized to the sojourner's treatment (Leviticus 19:34; Deuteronomy 10:19: "for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt"); the creation ground (Job 31:15) is fully universal in form. Control test: the land law's ground ("the land is mine," v. 23) is complete in itself and waits on no extension -- correct (no accommodation signal). Divorce: the "from the beginning" norm is universal in form and Jesus generalizes it back over the permission -- correct. Sustained, as a synthesis criterion: it reads the shape of the whole canon and cannot be discharged from one chapter. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM]

**C6. Canonical trajectory and reception.** Do the canon's later voices treat the practice as under judgment or as transcended, and never appeal to the permission as a norm? Derivation and data: Jeremiah 34 claims the release law as God's own covenant word from the Exodus (34:13-14) and treats re-enslavement after proclaimed liberty as profanation of God's name and covenant transgression, punished by judgment (34:16-22) -- its direct scope is Hebrew slaves, and the extension is inference; Isaiah 58:6-7 defines the fast God chooses as loosing the yoke and letting the oppressed go free (shallach chophshim), with no ethnic restriction stated; the apostolic reception never cites the permission texts as normative (the manuscript's citational argument, received here as its argument, SECONDARY to the manuscript and not independently audited in this note), while manstealing stands condemned in the apostolic vice list (1 Timothy 1:10, andrapodistai; the ESV apparatus cross-references 1 Timothy 1:10 at Exodus 21:16); and the rabbinic reception's greatest codifier states the permission's letter and then overruns it on the Torah's own moral quality (Maimonides, Laws of Slaves 9:8, via Sommer: "Scripture turns these people over to slavery, but not to disgrace"). Control test: the Jubilee is celebrated, not transcended (Isaiah 61:1) -- correct; divorce is closed down by Jesus -- correct. Sustained. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH]

**C7. Eschatological absence -- tested and demoted.** Candidate: an institution absent from the canon's final ordering (no slavery in Isaiah 65-66 or Revelation 21-22) is thereby marked as provisional. The marriage control refutes C7 as a discriminator: marriage is the endorsed control, yet "in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage" (Matthew 22:30). Eschatological absence proves at most provisionality-in-this-age, which endorsed goods can share. C7 is retained only as weak corroboration and may not be cited as evidence of disapproval. [Philosophical conjecture as originally framed; the refutation is Explicit text, HIGH]

**Criteria count: seven developed; six sustained as discriminators (C1-C6, with C3 corroborating only); one demoted (C7).** This is the criteria set T-VI.2 required. [HIGH as a report of this note's own construction]

## Step 4: Application to Leviticus 25:44-46

- **C1 (etiology): partially met, canonically.** Leviticus 25 itself states no hardness-of-heart etiology for the foreign permission. What the text states is a contrast of grounds: Israelites cannot be held because God owns them by redemption (vv. 42, 55). The fallenness frame (slavery as a feature of the post-Fall order the Noahic age tolerates, Genesis 8:21's evil-from-youth acknowledgment, per the Noahic repair's narrower claim) is supplied by the canon, not by the chapter. Grade: met at the canonical level only. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM]
- **C2 (principle tension): met, and this is the strongest single datum.** The same Holiness corpus that grants the permission commands love of the sojourner as oneself (19:34) and one rule for sojourner and native (24:22), and the permission's own chapter grounds its protections in a redemption logic whose premise (persons held absolutely by God are not ownable by men) is left ungeneralized. The tension is intra-corpus, specific, and unresolved by the text itself. Grade: met. [Necessary inference, HIGH]
- **C3 (subversive protections): met, as corroboration.** Injury manumission (Exodus 21:26-27) and fugitive asylum (Deuteronomy 23:15-16) are stated without ethnic restriction and apply on their terms to the foreign slave of Leviticus 25; Sabbath rest and Passover inclusion likewise. Against them stands Exodus 21:21's kaspo clause, which shows the property frame persisting alongside. Grade: met as tension-evidence, not as negation of the property status F1 establishes. [Necessary inference, MEDIUM]
- **C4 (permission grammar): met.** Verses 44-46 are concessive in form ("as for your male and female slaves whom you may have... you may buy... you may bequeath"), embedded in a chapter of commands running the other way, accompanied by no command to acquire slaves, no rationale praising foreign enslavement, and no cultic or creational institution of it; and the canon bans the acquisition act of manstealing on pain of death (Exodus 21:16, of "a man" without ethnic qualification; Deuteronomy 24:7 states the case for an Israelite victim). The law regulates a market it did not create and forbids Israel to supply it by capture. Grade: met. [Explicit text / Necessary inference, HIGH]
- **C5 (ungeneralized rationale): met.** The redemption ground that absolutely protects the Israelite is generalized by the corpus to the sojourner's treatment (Leviticus 19:34; Deuteronomy 10:18-19) and underwritten universally by the common-Maker premise (Job 31:15), yet it is not generalized to the foreign slave's status in Leviticus 25. On C5 this ungeneralized state is the signature of a staging post. Grade: met. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM]
- **C6 (trajectory and reception): met.** Jeremiah 34 enforces release as covenant (scope: Hebrews; extension by inference), Isaiah 58:6-7 states yoke-breaking without ethnic limit, Isaiah 61:1 takes up the Jubilee's own deror for the eschatological proclamation, the apostolic writings never wield the permission as a norm and condemn enslavers, and Maimonides keeps the letter while declaring the Torah's own quality of lovingkindness against its harsh use. No canonical voice celebrates or reaffirms the foreign permission. Grade: met. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH]
- **C7: not used** (demoted, Step 3).

**The asymmetry under the criteria.** F2's threefold asymmetry is exactly what the criteria must explain, and their explanation is specific: the asymmetry is the shape accommodation takes when a universal principle is being staged through a covenant nation whose own members carry an absolute, redemption-grounded protection that the surrounding classes do not yet carry. The criteria do not soften F1 or F3. They locate verses 44-46 as the point of maximum distance between the corpus's stated principle and its permitted practice, which is precisely where the accommodation category, as chartered by Matthew 19:8, predicts such permissions to sit. This location claim is the note's central synthesis and should be quoted as such, not paraphrased into something stronger. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH]

## Step 5: The two classifications in strongest form

**The endorsement / permanence reading, strongest form.** (i) Deliberate drafting: the Holiness legislator draws the ruthlessness line at the ethnic boundary three times in one unit (vv. 43, 46, 53) and classifies foreign slaves with the household's only other perpetual asset class, inherited land (achuzzah, nachalah). A legislator capable of verse 42's absolute protection could have written it universally; the restriction is the meaning. (ii) No canonical revocation: no prophet, and neither Jesus nor Paul, ever names foreign slavery under Leviticus 25 as sin or revokes the permission; Jeremiah 34, the canon's one enforcement action on release, confines itself to Hebrew slaves, which on this reading confirms the boundary rather than crossing it. (iii) Reception: Maimonides states the permission as din, the law, including bepharekh labor; the tradition's ambivalence ("but lovingkindness says otherwise") presupposes that the letter really permits what kindness restrains. (iv) Historical: within the Holiness Code's social program, foreign slavery is a standing feature of the ordered world, and permanence vocabulary (le'olam) is the vocabulary the code elsewhere uses for God's own irrevocable gifts (Genesis 17:8, 13); reading an abolitionist intent into the chapter imports a conclusion the text never states. On this reading, "accommodation" is a theological verdict later readers pass on the text, not a classification the text supports, and the honest scholarly description is that the code endorses the institution for its world. [Stated as the opposing construction, HIGH that these are its strongest available arguments]

**The accommodation reading, strongest form.** (i) The category is not imported: Jesus himself distinguishes Mosaic permission from divine institution using exactly this evidence-pattern (etiology, permission grammar, prior norm), so classifying a permission as non-instituted is a canonical act before it is a modern one (Matthew 19:7-8). (ii) The corpus supplies the prior norm in the permission's own stratum: sojourner-love as oneself (Leviticus 19:34), one rule for sojourner and native (Leviticus 24:22), God's own love of the sojourner as the model (Deuteronomy 10:18-19). (iii) The system's protections cross the permission's own line (injury manumission, fugitive asylum, Sabbath, Passover), and its acquisition side is fenced by a capital ban on manstealing; the one thing the law never does is command, institute, or praise the enslavement of anyone. (iv) The reception runs one way: release enforced as covenant (Jeremiah 34, extended by the deror logic the chapter itself universalizes in wording at v. 10), yoke-breaking as God's chosen fast (Isaiah 58), the permission never once wielded as a norm by the apostles, and the codifier of the tradition moralizing against its harsh letter. (v) The manuscript's deepest instinct is right and survives the binary's fall: a permission that contradicts the system's own stated principle, hedged by subversive protections, never instituted, and never reaffirmed, is doing different theological work than an endorsement, whatever its original social function. [Stated as this note's construction, Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH]

**Where the endorsement reading scores against the criteria, stated plainly.** It wins the purely exegetical question about the chapter's own horizon: within Leviticus 25's world, foreign slavery is legislated without embarrassment, apology, or etiology, and C1 is met only canonically, C3 only corroboratively. Any Part VI sentence implying the chapter itself signals its own provisionality beyond the deror wording of verse 10 and the ungeneralized rationale of verses 42 and 55 overstates the exegesis. The accommodation verdict is a canonical-theological classification, and its strength depends on the treatise's confessed hermeneutic (Scripture interpreting Scripture, principle over permission per Matthew 19:8), not on a discovery inside verses 44-46. [HIGH]

## Step 6: Verdict and the Part VI claim set

**Verdict.** Classifying Leviticus 25:44-46 as accommodation rather than endorsement is SUSTAINED under the criteria C1-C6, as a theological synthesis at MEDIUM-HIGH confidence. It is NOT sustained as a claim about the text's explicit self-presentation (at that level the classification is LOW and must never be argued). The correct two-level statement for Part VI is: exegetically, verses 44-46 grant permanent, heritable property in foreign slaves without stated qualification; canonically, the permission meets the accommodation criteria the canon itself supplies, and the treatise classifies it accordingly. Thesis T-VI.2 may proceed on that basis; the block is lifted by the existence of these criteria, not by their verdict being unanimous. [Theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH]

**Part VI MAY claim:**
1. Verses 44-46 are permission, not institution or command; the canon commands no enslavement and bans manstealing capitally (Exodus 21:16; Deuteronomy 24:7). [Explicit text]
2. The permission stands in documented intra-corpus tension with Leviticus 19:34, Leviticus 24:22, and Deuteronomy 10:18-19, and with the ungeneralized redemption rationale of its own verses 42 and 55. [Explicit text / Necessary inference]
3. The foreign slave of Leviticus 25 remains under protections stated without ethnic restriction (Exodus 21:26-27; Deuteronomy 23:15-16; Exodus 20:10; Exodus 12:44), which stand in tension with the property status verses 45-46 grant. [Necessary inference, HIGH]
4. Under criteria C1-C6 (Matthew 19:8 supplying the category), the classification "accommodation" is warranted as canonical theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH, with the asymmetry of F2 named as the accommodation's hardest datum, not explained away. [Theological synthesis]
5. The Noahic age-description (from the repair): the permission belongs to an age in which God's ordinary government is patient and discriminating rather than universally destructive, which is why the question "why permit at all" has a covenantal answer that does not touch the classification itself. [Theological synthesis, per research-noahic-binary.md]

**Part VI MUST NOT claim:**
1. That Leviticus 25 limits foreign servitude in term, extends the Jubilee release to foreign slaves by its own provisions, or prohibits ruthless rule over them. All three are false in terms (F2, F3); Maimonides' din reading stands as the reception's witness against softening the letter.
2. That the accommodation classification is explicit in, or exegetically compelled by, verses 44-46 themselves, or that it carries HIGH confidence as exegesis.
3. That cross-ethnic protections negate the chattel character of the status granted (the manuscript's "incomprehensible in a true chattel system" line, corrected in Step 1): protections mitigate and, across the canon, subvert; they do not annul the achuzzah classification at law.
4. That the ethnic asymmetry is adequately explained as covenantal bookkeeping about land allotments (the manuscript's softer framing). The covenant structure explains the asymmetry's mechanics (a redemption-protected nation in a slaveholding world); it does not discharge its moral remainder, which the accommodation classification itself presupposes: were there no remainder, there would be nothing to accommodate.
5. That Jeremiah 34 or Isaiah 58 directly address foreign slaves. Jeremiah 34's scope is Hebrew slaves; Isaiah 58's oppressed are unspecified. Both function in C6 as trajectory evidence by inference and must be cited as such.
6. That abolition is commanded by the text. It is entailed, on the treatise's argument, by the principle the text states (Leviticus 19:18, 34 as received in Matthew 22 and Romans 13); the entailment is the treatise's synthesis and must wear that tag.

## Step 7: Interaction statement

**With the Noahic repair.** The dependency runs one way only.
