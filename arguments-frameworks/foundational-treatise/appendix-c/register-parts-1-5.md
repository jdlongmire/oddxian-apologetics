## Part I: Theology Proper - Eternal Triune Communion as the Moral Archetype

Part I's argument: God's eternal triune life is itself loving communion; that life, not an abstract standard above God or a bare divine fiat, is the moral archetype, which grounds the treatise's Euthyphro resolution and supplies the definition against which sin (Part IV) and consummation (Part VIII) are measured. The research notes seed almost nothing for Part I directly; most entries below are work-package requirements not yet opened, and they are registered honestly as such.

### Classical counter-position

1. **Plato, Euthyphro.** Edition of record: not yet fixed (a standard Greek text with a named translation must be chosen at first consultation). Take/contest: the dialogue poses the dilemma Part I must resolve - whether the good is good because God wills it, or God wills it because it is good. The treatise contests both horns as exhaustive: the good is God's own triune character, neither external to him nor arbitrary in him. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

### Councils and creeds

2. **Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (325/381).** Edition of record: not yet fixed (conciliar text to be cited from a named collection, e.g. the acts as printed in a standard conciliar edition). Take/contest: the creed's homoousios grammar is the boundary within which Part I states eternal triune communion; the treatise takes its Trinitarian realism from the creed and does not contest it. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.
3. **Chalcedonian Definition (451).** Edition of record: the Definition as received and restated in the acts of Constantinople III (Schaff, Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, second series, vol. 14), where its predicate "in all things like us, sin only excepted" is carried forward. Take/contest: Part I takes the one-person, two-natures grammar as fixed background for speaking of the Son's eternal communion with the Father; the Definition's own text was not opened separately from the Constantinople III acts in Phase 1. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

### Patristic

4. **Athanasius, Orationes contra Arianos; De incarnatione.** Edition of record: not yet fixed. Take/contest: the treatise takes from Athanasius the anti-Arian insistence that the Son is fully God by nature, without which "eternal triune communion" collapses into a society of unequals; nothing in Part I contests him. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.
5. **Augustine, De Trinitate.** Edition of record: not yet fixed (Latin critical text in CCL 50/50A by standard bibliography; not verified from a source opened in Phase 1). Take/contest: the treatise takes the Western grammar of one essence and three persons, and Augustine's psychological analogies are engaged critically where they risk subordinating communion to interiority; Augustine's verified loci in the research notes (City of God, Enchiridion, De correptione et gratia) are registered under Part IV, where they were actually read. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.
6. **Irenaeus, Adversus Haereses.** Edition of record: not yet fixed. Take/contest: named by the work package for recapitulation; for Part I his contribution is the eternal Word's unbroken communion with the Father as the pattern creation images; his recapitulation argument proper is load-bearing in Part VIII and is registered here only as background. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

### Medieval

7. **Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I (on God and the Trinity).** Edition of record: not yet fixed. Take/contest: the treatise takes the classical grammar of divine simplicity and processions as an interlocutor for stating eternal communion without composition, and contests any reading of simplicity that would make the persons' mutual love unreal; Aquinas on providence and evil is registered under Part V. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

### Reformation and post-Reformation Reformed

8. **Westminster Confession of Faith, ch. 2 (Of God, and of the Holy Trinity).** Edition of record: the standard text. Take/contest: the chapter is Part I's confessional boundary marker (one God, three persons, the divine perfections); the Phase 1 notes verified Westminster texts for chs. 7 and 8 and the Larger Catechism (registered under Parts II and V) but did not open ch. 2 for this register. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

### Modern and contemporary

9. **Jonathan Edwards, The Nature of True Virtue.** Edition of record: not yet fixed (Yale Works of Jonathan Edwards by standard bibliography; volume not verified from a source opened in Phase 1). Take/contest: Edwards defines true virtue as benevolence to Being in general; Part I takes his consent-to-Being frame as the nearest early-modern analogue to its archetype claim and contests it where its object (Being in general) is less personal than the triune communion the treatise names. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.
10. **Herman Bavinck, Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2 (God and Creation).** Edition of record: not yet fixed (English ed. John Bolt, Baker, 2003-2008, by standard bibliography; not verified in Phase 1). Take/contest: Bavinck's doctrine of God is the treatise's principal Dutch Reformed interlocutor for stating the Trinity as the archetype of all communion; what must be checked is his exact treatment of the divine attributes as the moral pattern for creatures. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.
11. **Geerhardus Vos, Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 1 (Theology Proper).** Edition of record: not yet fixed (English ed. Richard B. Gaffin Jr., Lexham, by standard bibliography; not verified in Phase 1). Take/contest: Vos is registered for his ordering of the doctrine of God and his covenantal framing of revelation; what must be checked is whether his theology proper supplies or resists the treatise's archetype formulation. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.
12. **Cornelius Van Til, The Defense of the Faith; Christian Apologetics.** Edition of record: not yet fixed. Take/contest: named by the work package for the presuppositional framing and no-neutrality rule that Part I's apologetic deployment assumes: the triune God is not argued to from neutral ground but stated as the precondition of moral intelligibility. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

## Part II: The Eternal Covenant and God's Good Decree

Part II's argument: God's one eternal decree is stated covenantally in the pactum salutis, the Father's giving of the elect to the Son and the Son's undertaking of suretyship, formally distinct from the decree yet one with it in effect; election is in Christ, corporate in form and individual by necessary entailment (study 06); the governing premise drawn from Romans 8:28-30 does not depend on the ho theos variant (study 05); Hebrews 13:20 contributes to the pactum only through a mediating chain, not as a classical proof text (study 10 and the pactum note).

### Councils and creeds (confessional status of the pactum)

1. **Westminster Confession of Faith 7.3 and 8.1.** Edition of record: the standard text, as cited in the pactum research note from the tradition's own references. Take/contest: the Confession speaks of the covenant of grace and of Christ appointed Mediator "in his eternal purpose," and does NOT name a covenant of redemption; Part II takes this silence as a boundary: the pactum may be taught as good and necessary consequence, never quoted as confessed by name. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (the pactum note, citing the standard texts as reproduced in Muller and the RTS history; not re-verified against a critical edition in Phase 1).
2. **Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 31.** Edition of record: the standard text, as cited in the pactum note. Take/contest: the covenant of grace was "made with Christ as the second Adam, and in him with all the elect as his seed" - the closest Westminster text to the pactum; Part II takes it as stating the covenant with Christ, not a separate intra-Trinitarian pact. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (the pactum note, as above).
3. **Savoy Declaration (1658), ch. 8, par. 1.** Edition of record: the text as quoted in the RTS history. Take/contest: Savoy amends Westminster by adding "according to a covenant made between them both"; Part II takes this as the strongest confessional attestation of the pactum, and notes it is post-Westminster. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (RTS history quotation, via the pactum note).
4. **Second London Baptist Confession (1689), ch. 8, par. 1.** Edition of record: the text as quoted in the RTS history. Take/contest: the same amendment as Savoy; registered for the same reason and with the same weight. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (RTS history quotation, via the pactum note).
5. **Helvetic Consensus Formula (1675).** Edition of record: not yet fixed. Take/contest: listed by the RTS history among documents showing the doctrine's later seventeenth-century spread; the pactum note expressly did not verify its wording, and Part II may not cite it until the text is opened. Status: NOT YET CONSULTED.

### Patristic and pre-Reformed antecedents

6. **Martin Luther, Lectures on Galatians (1519).** Content as reported: Christ as immortal God "made a pactum" and as about-to-be-mortal "made a testamentum." Take/contest: registered as the earliest antecedent in Muller's genealogy of the pactum; Part II takes it as background evidence that the covenantal packaging of the decree predates the federal theologians, not as a formulation of the doctrine. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Muller, "Toward the Pactum Salutis," 2007).
7. **Johannes Oecolampadius, on Isaiah 54:10 and 55:3 (1525).** Content as reported by Muller. Take/contest: antecedent covenantal reading of the Isaianic everlasting covenant; background only. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Muller, 2007).
8. **Theodore Beza, Latin New Testament (1567), at Luke 22:29.** Content: Beza rendered diatithemai covenantally ("made a covenant with") against the Vulgate's dispono. Take/contest: Part II takes the rendering as a load-bearing link in the tradition's proof structure for Luke 22:29 and flags that the covenantal reading of the verse partly depends on it. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Muller, 2007).
9. **Caspar Olevianus, De substantia foederis gratuiti inter Deum et electos (Geneva, 1585), 1.2.** Content as reported: the Son, appointed by God as Mediator, becomes guarantor on two counts for those the Father has given him; eternal generation as an element of the substance of the covenant of grace (De substantia pp. 23, 63, 106 in Muller's citations). Take/contest: Part II cites Olevianus as an early form and antecedent only; he does not use pactum salutis or foedus redemptionis or the two-pact structure, and Heppe and Schrenk's "fully present" claim is an overstatement on Bierma's reading (German Calvinism in the Confessional Age, pp. 107-112). Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Muller, 2007; Bierma as reported by Muller; R. Scott Clark's summaries).

## Part III: Creation - Very Good, Upright and Mutable

Part III's argument: Genesis 1:31's tov meod pronounces creation fitting and excellent of its kind; it does not lexically encode incorruptible, immutable, or consummated (study 01). Eden was upright and mutable; the wholeness vocabulary Scripture possessed (tamim, shalem) is not used of creation in Genesis 1-2. The two-states account (very good and mutable against perfected and confirmed) is theological synthesis at MEDIUM, and Eden-as-defective is rejected.

### Councils and creeds

1. **Westminster Confession of Faith 4.2 and 6.1.** Edition of record: the standard text, as cited in study 01. Take/contest: the traditional Reformed reading - man created in knowledge, righteousness, and holiness, with immortality conditional on obedience - is accepted in substance by study 01, with the narrowing note that those attributes rest on the wider canon (Eccl 7:29; Eph 4:24; Col 3:10) and that conditional immortality itself confirms Edenic mutability. Status: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (study 01's citation; the standards text was not separately opened for this Part).

## Part IV: Fall and Sin - Closed Self-Interest as Root, Tested

Part IV's argument: sin's root is the self closed in on itself (incurvatus in se) against triune loving communion, stated - per the formulation JD adopted on 2026-11-01 - at the level of root condition and orientation, with four qualifications (Q1 condition, not motive; Q2 closure primary, with grasping, defensive, inert, and arbitral modes named; Q3 self-preservation is a created good, sinful only when absolutized; Q4 the arbitral mode plus a motive-opacity rule). Drafting Part IV in the unqualified universal-motive form is barred by Leviticus 4-5 and Numbers 15. Augustine's fourfold state (posse scheme of De correptione et gratia 12.33) supplies Part IV's anthropological frame; the register caution is that the tag non posse non peccare was NOT found verbatim in any Augustinian text opened - it is the later tradition's label and must not be printed as Augustine's own words.

## Part V: The Decree and the Problem of Evil

Part V's argument: God's decree is good and governs all things (Eph 1:11 supplies the domain, not the mode - study 06); intention over evil is asymmetrical (the one verb of Gen 50:20 governs differently constructed clauses - study 03); the governing premise of Romans 8:28-30 is variant-independent (study 05); divine regret and grief are real affections without man-like relenting (Gen 6:5-7 with Num 23:19 and 1 Sam 15 - study 02); and Deuteronomy 29:29 marks the epistemic boundary for handling the decree - it does not address any particular evil, and the no-silencing-lament guardrail must be carried on the Psalms and Job, not on this verse (study 04). Bare permission is rejected with Calvin (Institutes 1.18). The boundary subsection registers the impeccability material where Part V's decree treatment meets Part VII: the decree's guarantee that the Mediator cannot fail.

### Boundary with Part VII: the decree and the Mediator's impeccability

Part V's decree treatment requires that the Mediator's success be guaranteed, not merely foreseen; the following entries are the register base for that joint, fully developed in Part VII's register.

Take/contest: the council confesses two natural wills and two natural operations, the human will following and subject to the divine, "although deified... not suppressed, but rather preserved"; its own scriptural anchor in the Definition is John 6:38, with Luke 22:42 entering through Agatho's letter and its Athanasian florilegium. Status: CONSULTED DIRECTLY.
27. **Francis Turretin, Institutes, Thirteenth Topic, Q. VII, sec. XIV (vol. 2, pp. 320-321), with XIII.v, XI-XIII.** Edition of record: Giger translation, ed. 28. **W. G. T. Take/contest: the weightiest intra-Reformed dissent; study 09 declines it on grammar and on Hebrews' own sympathy logic ("temptation implies the possibility of sin" is a philosophical premise, not an exegetical finding), while retaining what it rightly protects (the temptations and their cost were real).
