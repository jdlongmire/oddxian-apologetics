## Part VII: Christology

Part VII's spine is the control-text triad of studies 07, 08, and 09 (Luke 22:42, John 6:38, Hebrews 4:15), the conciliar confession of two natural wills, and the impeccability inference. The register preserves the three dogmatic tiers of research-two-wills.md: Tier A conciliar dogma, Tier B confessional boundary, Tier C theological inference. Study 09 splits the impeccability tag: MEDIUM as an inference from Hebrews 4:15 alone, HIGH as theological synthesis on the full premise set; the two-wills note holds MEDIUM with stated raise conditions. Part VII must print one calibration; that reconciliation is a Phase 2 audit item, flagged in the open checks.

## Part VIII: Consummation

Part VIII's spine is study 11 (Revelation 21-22) joined to the Augustinian four-state scheme: Eden mutable (posse peccare et posse non peccare), glory confirmed (non posse peccare). Study 11's verdicts control the register: the state's permanence is a necessary inference rated HIGH; the label "confirmed, non posse peccare" applied to the saints' wills is theological synthesis, MEDIUM-HIGH, reserved to Phase 2 with the Augustinian scheme. The honesty flag from the Augustine note governs every Augustinian entry here: the tag non posse non peccare was not found verbatim in any Augustinian text opened; it is the later tradition's label for the fallen condition Augustine describes in prose, and the treatise must not print it as Augustine's own words.

## Part X: Objections Register

Part X registers the objectors themselves as the interlocutors. Each entry states the objection in its strongest form, names where the treatise answers it, and carries the same verification discipline. Objections whose strongest form rests on a generic reading in a Phase 1 study, with no primary objector text opened, are marked NOT YET CONSULTED for the primary literature; the study's own adjudication is the answer of record until then.

Entry X-1. Charles Hodge, the temptation objection (Systematic Theology, vol. 2, p. 457). Strongest form, in his words as quoted: "Temptation implies the possibility of sin. If from the constitution of his person it was impossible for Christ to sin, then his temptation was unreal and without effect, and he cannot sympathize with his people." The objection has two prongs: a definition (temptation analytically implies the possibility of succumbing) and a pastoral consequence (an impeccable Christ cannot sympathize, against Hebrews 4:15). Answered in: study 09, sections 3.1, 3.4, and 5.1 and findings 3 and 9 (peirazo encodes nothing about openness of outcome; the epistle grounds sympathy in completed experience, not in a capacity to fall; the premise is philosophical, not exegetical), and research-two-wills.md, section 3.3.4 (Shedd's reply: an army that cannot be conquered can still be attacked; he bore temptation's full cumulative force by never yielding; the elect-angels analogy). Honesty flag: Hodge is mediated, not read directly (see entry VII-15). Verification: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Walvoord's survey; Shedd's footnote; Willmington).

Entry X-2. Brian J. Abasciano, the corporate-election definition ("corporate election refers to the choice of a group, which entails the choice of its individual members by virtue of their membership in the group; individuals are not elected as individuals directly, but secondarily as members of the elect group"), from "Clearing Up Misconceptions About Corporate Election," as excerpted in the Society of Evangelical Arminians' corporate election excerpts. Strongest form: Ephesians 1 affirms the church's election in Christ without any hint of God's choice of individuals to populate that body. Answered in: study 06, section 5.1 (the register is accepted, the strong negative claim declined, on three grammatical facts: exelexato middle voice with a personal object; the aorist predestining acts dated pro kataboles kosmou with persons as objects; the we/you movement of 1:12-13 describes the blessing's arrival, not the elect set's constitution). Verification: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (the Society of Evangelical Arminians excerpt collection).

Entry X-3. William W. Klein, The New Chosen People (1990) and "Is Corporate Election Merely Virtual Election?" (thesis: "we can indeed speak of God's choice of the church in Christ without also implying that God specifically chose the individuals who will comprise that church"). Answered in: study 06, section 5.1, on the same three grammatical facts as entry X-2; the corporate reading must silently supply determinacy of membership from future faith, which relocates it into the conditional-foreknowledge objection (entry X-6). Verification: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Klein's article and book thesis as reproduced at evangelicalarminians.org).

Entry X-4. Klyne Snodgrass, Ephesians (NIV Application Commentary), as quoted by Klein: "election is primarily a corporate term... Individuals are not elected and then put in Christ. They are in Christ and therefore elect." Answered in: study 06, section 5.1 (the corporate form and en Christo location are adopted as the text's own; the inference that no individual choice is in view is declined). Verification: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (quoted by Klein and in the Society of Evangelical Arminians excerpts).

Entry X-5. Karl Barth, the Christological form of corporate election (Church Dogmatics II/2: Jesus Christ as both electing God and elected man, the community elect in him, individuals elect as members), as it bears on Ephesians 1 and, separately, Barth's pactum critique (entry X-9). Edition of record: Church Dogmatics was not consulted for study 06; Barth's form is described in its standard summary form and expressly not quoted there. Disposition: the Christological concentration is retained as the passage's own framing; Barth's further dogmatic steps on the extent of election and reprobation in Christ go beyond Ephesians 1 and are answered, if at all, at Part X level only after the primary text is opened. Verification: NOT YET CONSULTED.

Entry X-6. The conditional-foreknowledge objection to Ephesians 1 (God chose and predestined on the basis of foreseen faith; prothesis is the plan of salvation, not a determination of who believes). Strongest form: it guards human responsibility and the text's silence about reprobation, and reads the blessings (adoption, inheritance, holiness) as predestined to believers as such. Answered in: study 06, section 5.2 (declined: no condition is expressed where grounds are listed four times, all on God's side; the infinitive of 1:4 makes holiness the choosing's purpose, not its prerequisite; proginosko is absent from the passage). No primary conditionalist text was opened for the study; the objection is registered in the study's own strongest-form statement. Verification: NOT YET CONSULTED (primary literature).

Entry X-7. The open-theism objection from Genesis 6:5-7 (God learns, is surprised, and revises his plans; divine nacham shows real change of mind). Strongest form: the regret verbs are read at face value as evidence of new information and revised intention. Answered in: study 02, sections 3.1 and 5.1 and findings 1-3 and 5 (the text supplies no ignorance premise; the canon holds both poles in one chapter, 1 Samuel 15:11 and 35 with 15:29 and Numbers 23:19; what is denied is man-like relenting, lying, and caprice, while real regret and real grief, atsav in the Hithpael "to his heart," are affirmed; the metaphysical reconciliation is deferred to Part V). The pure-metaphor counter-reading is declined in the same place. Chisholm (1995) and Parunak (1975) were reached only through the NET textual notes and are not registered as consulted objectors or authorities. Verification: NOT YET CONSULTED (primary open-theist literature).

Entry X-9. Answered in: research-pactum-salutis.md, section 3.2 (the tradition's own formula, Owen via Swain: the one will distinctly applied to its distinct acts in the persons, not a distinction of sundry wills; a Brakel asks and answers the same question; the pactum is formally distinct from the decree, "in effect one with the decree of redemption," per Dickson, not a second decree). Verification: CONSULTED VIA MEDIATION (Swain, quoted in First Things (2017), "Triune Will and Triune Decree").

Entry X-10. Loonstra and Beckwith, the exegetical-thinness critique of the pactum (no text states an intra-Trinitarian covenant; the proof lists are collations of appointment, giving, sending, obedience, and reward texts, none of which says "covenant" in that sense). Answered in: research-pactum-salutis.md, sections 2, 3.2, and 4 (Muller's counter: a concerted collation by the era's methods, answering questions the decree alone did not, with antecedents in Luther's 1519 Galatians lectures, Oecolampadius 1525, Beza's 1567 rendering of Luke 22:29, Perkins, and Polanus; the treatise tags the pactum Theological synthesis, MEDIUM for scriptural explicitness, HIGH for traditional attestation, and does not present Zechariah 6:13 as a proof text or Hebrews 13:20 as a classical proof text, Hebrews 13:20 being absent from every classical list examined).
