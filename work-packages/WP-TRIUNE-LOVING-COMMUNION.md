## 1. Objective
Systematically codify, test, and integrate the thesis that God's eternal triune character establishes loving communion as the moral ideal, the foundation of covenantal purpose, and the interpretive framework for creation, sin, law, redemption, and consummation.

## 2. Canonical thesis (preserve verbatim)
> God's eternal triune character establishes the moral ideal: loving communion. Creation reveals its intended expression. The Fall explains its corruption and the true nature of sin: closed self-interest. Law governs humanity amid that corruption. Christ reveals and accomplishes its restoration.

## 3. Foundational definitions and claims
- **Triune loving communion:** Eternal, self-sufficient interpersonal communion of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit within the one divine being. Creation adds no necessary perfection to God.
- **Imago Dei:** Humanity created with dignity and a relational vocation to reflect God's loving communion. Self-love is a proper good when ordered toward God and neighbor.
- **Closed self-interest:** Self elevated into the ultimate practical reference point, displacing rightful love of God and neighbor. Proposed universal root of sin, distinct from the proximate motive or observable expression of individual sins.
- **Disordered expressions:** Pride, greed, lust, hatred, idolatry, neglect, and other sins are manifestations of closed self-interest, not independent root causes.
- **Law:** Revelation of righteousness and moral governance, including restraint and accommodation amid fallen conditions; distinguish eternal moral character from temporally administered regulations.
- **Redemption and consummation:** Christ decisively accomplishes reconciliation; the Spirit transforms believers; communion is consummated in the new creation.
- **Eternal covenant:** Investigate the relation of eternal divine purpose, the covenant of redemption (*pactum salutis*), new covenant, and consummated communion. Distinguish explicit texts from systematic inference.

## 4. Relational parsimony thesis
**User formulation:** “One is regressive. Two is obsessive. Three is expressive.”

Develop as a philosophical model, not as an independent proof of the Trinity:
1. One: reflexive/self-directed relationality.
2. Two: reciprocal love within a *closed dyad*; do not claim that two persons are incapable of welcoming others.
3. Three: shared communion as a distinct triadic relational category; the minimum number for a relation involving three distinct persons.
4. **Expression:** Enables expansion of loving communion to others without requiring creation to complete God.
5. **Epistemic order:** God's self-revelation establishes triune identity; parsimony examines coherence and minimal sufficiency. Do not claim a logical demonstration that exactly three divine persons are necessary solely from relational mathematics.
6. Evaluate the stronger claim that the triad transforms reciprocity into a categorically different kind of communion, and objections concerning exclusivity, fourth persons, and equivocation between divine and creaturely personhood.
