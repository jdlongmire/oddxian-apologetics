## Abstract and governing proposition

The Edenic Covenant and Human Calling (ST-2, Creation) teaches that life with God before the Fall was already covenantal in substance: a gracious arrangement in which God blessed humanity, gave it a home, a calling, a single clear command, a promise of life with a warning of death, marriage companionship, and the pattern of Sabbath rest. The narrative substantiates the plate in the order Genesis gives it: blessing and commission (panel 1), placement (panel 2), vocation (panel 3), generosity and command (panels 4 and 5), promise, warning, and headship (panel 6), the tree of life (panel 7), marriage (panel 8), and Sabbath (panel 9). Panel 10 summarizes. The volume stands between BWM-IS-06 (Humanity in God's Image), whose panel 7 first states the work-and-keep commission, and BWM-IS-08 (The Fall: From Communion to Rebellion), which takes up the breach of this command. Two disciplines govern the whole volume. First, the name covenant of works is the Westminster Confession's formulation (WCF 7.2; 19.1), received here as a historical formulation with HIGH confidence as a report of the confession; Genesis itself does not apply the word covenant to Eden, and the plate does not pretend otherwise. Second, Adam's standing was real: upright, aided, and able to stand, yet unconfirmed, per the treatise's axiom III.5. Nothing in this volume teaches that the Fall was necessary in Adam's constitution, or that Eden was defective.

## Biblical grounding

- Genesis 1:28 is the commission text: God's blessing spoken over mankind, with fruitfulness, filling, subduing, and dominion as its content. Blessing precedes task; the verbs are gifts before they are duties.
- Genesis 2:8 and 2:15 give placement and vocation: God plants the garden, puts the man there, and gives the garden to be worked and kept. The work-and-keep pair (avad and shamar) is the pair later used for sanctuary service, which is the basis, and the limit, of the priestly colouring discussed below.
- Genesis 2:16-17 gives the command in its full form: a wide permission (every tree), a single prohibition (one tree), and a stated sanction (death in the day of eating). The life side of the sanction is by implication and by the tree of life's presence, and the narrative registers it as the confessional formulation states it, not as a sentence Genesis quotes in positive form.
- Genesis 2:9 places the tree of life in the midst of the garden alongside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Genesis 3:22-24, read back only as the narrative's own sequel, confirms that access to the tree of life is the point at which obedience, breach, and confirmed life meet; this volume cites that sequel for the tree's significance and for nothing about the Fall itself, which is BWM-IS-08's subject.
- Genesis 2:18, 2:23-24 gives companionship: the one pre-Fall not good (aloneness, treatise Part III sec. 5.1) supplied inside the creation order, and marriage instituted as one-flesh union.
- Genesis 2:2-3 gives the Sabbath pattern: God finishes, rests, blesses the seventh day, and makes it holy. The calling is framed by God's rest from the beginning.
- Romans 5:12-19 and 1 Corinthians 15:45-49 supply the headship consequence retrospectively: Adam acts as a public head, so his obedience or disobedience cannot remain a private matter. This is canonical consequence, not a claim that Genesis 2 uses federal language.
- Hosea 6:7 ("like Adam they transgressed the covenant," ESV) is the one text that joins Adam and covenant in a single clause. Its rendering is contested (like Adam, at Adam as a place, or like men), so this volume records it as supporting and disputed, and rests no panel claim on it.

## Doctrinal derivation

Premises. (1) God initiates every element: blessing, garden, placement, permission, command, helper, and rest are all His acts before they are human responses (Genesis 1:28; 2:8, 15-18). (2) The command is morally serious and its sanction is stated, not implied (Genesis 2:17). (3) Adam's state is upright and mutable (Ecclesiastes 7:29; treatise axiom III.5): he received an aid of the without-which kind, could forsake it, had not received constancy to persevere, and might have stood (Calvin, Institutes 1.15.8, as reported in treatise Part III sec. 7.2, HIGH as a report). (4) Adam acts as head, so the arrangement's outcome bears on his posterity (Romans 5:12-19; WCF 7.2: in him to his posterity).

Consequences. Calling precedes curse: work, marriage, and rule are creation gifts, so none of them can be read as products of sin. Generosity precedes prohibition in the text's own order, so the command cannot fairly be pictured as a grudging restriction; one tree is withheld inside a garden given whole. And because confirmation was not yet received, the arrangement is genuinely open at the human side: obedience was possible, which is what makes the coming breach culpable rather than mechanical (treatise Part III sec. 8.3).

Synthesis, labeled as synthesis. Three elements of the plate are synthesis at MEDIUM, and they are carried at that grade. (a) The priestly colouring of work-and-keep (panel 3 caption tradition, carried from BWM-IS-06 panel 7): the avad and shamar pair's sanctuary use supports the colouring, per treatise Part III sec. 5.3, but Genesis 2 does not call the garden a temple in so many words. (b) The tree of life as the sign of confirmed life through obedience (panel 7): Genesis places the tree, and Genesis 3 guards it after the breach; the confirmation framing joins those facts to the treatise's two-states synthesis (axiom III.3, MEDIUM from study 01's side, MEDIUM-HIGH as the joint assessment with study 11). (c) Sabbath rest as the calling's goal (panel 9): Genesis institutes the pattern; the goal framing reads it with the canon's rest arc and treatise Part VIII, and is not a lexical claim about Genesis 2:2-3 alone.

Objections and limits. The strongest objection to the volume's title is that Genesis never calls Eden a covenant. The objection is exegetically correct about the word, and this narrative concedes it in the abstract: the claim is about substance (a divinely initiated bond with stipulation, promise, warning, and headship), stated in the confession's later vocabulary. JD ruled on the title on exactly those terms on 2026-11-13 (see Editorial record): the Edenic covenant position is his held position, and the title names the covenant Edenic rather than Original, Original belonging to the eternal covenant. A second objection, that probation language makes God the author of a rigged test, is answered by premise (3): the aid was real and the standing was possible, per treatise Part III sec. 8.3. Vos and Bavinck on Eden's pre-redemptive eschatology stand at NOT YET CONSULTED in the treatise Appendix C register (entries 12 and 13 for Part III); no originality claim is made for this volume's arrangement, and neither is cited for anything here.
