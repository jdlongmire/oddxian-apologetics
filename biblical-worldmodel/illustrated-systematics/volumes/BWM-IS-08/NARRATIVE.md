## Abstract and governing proposition

The Fall: From Communion to Rebellion (ST-3, Fall and Sin) teaches that the breach narrated in Genesis 3 is the breach of the Edenic covenant arrangement set out in BWM-IS-07: the blessed communion, placement, calling, command, promise, and warning of that volume are the order this volume shows broken, panel by panel, in the sequence Genesis itself gives. The narrative substantiates the plate in that order: the serpent's question (panel 1), Eve's recounting of the command (panel 2), the serpent's denial and offer (panel 3), the tree re-judged by desire (panel 4), the eating and giving (panel 5), opened eyes and shame (panel 6), hiding and fear (panel 7), blame under questioning (panel 8), judgments with the seed promise inside them (panel 9), and the chapter's own completion in mercy and exile (Genesis 3:20-24, carried in the Biblical grounding below). Panel 10 is the plate's summary checklist, closing on the seed promise of Genesis 3:15, and adds no new claims beyond its texts. The volume stands between BWM-IS-07 (The Edenic Covenant and Human Calling), whose arrangement is here breached, and BWM-IS-09 (The Root of Sin), which takes up the root claim in its full form, with BWM-IS-10 (Corruption and Death) following on the inherited condition and its consequence.

Two disciplines govern the whole volume. First, the root-of-sin thesis in its full qualified form, closed self-interest as the root of sin, labeled as synthesis, is BWM-IS-09's subject, not this volume's. This volume narrates the Fall itself and registers the root claim only at the grade the architecture plate assigns it, Good Consequence / synthesis, never as a motive asserted inside every act. That restraint follows treatise Part IV sec. 1 and its discipline: the unqualified form, every sin derives from a self-interested motive, was tested against the hardest classes of case and failed, and drafting in the unqualified form is barred as a conclusion of evidence, not as a stylistic preference. What this volume does assert on the root is thesis T-IV.3, at the grade stated below: the fall was man's own act and fault, its root pride, the evil will preceding the evil deed. Second, Genesis 3:15 is the first promise of the seed, and, per JD's ruling of 2026-11-13 recorded in the BWM-IS-07 narrative, it is the point where the lapsarian covenant of grace is set at the Fall, sealed by death (Hebrews 9:15). That framing is labeled here as JD's held position / synthesis, at MEDIUM, and no panel claim rests on it at a higher grade.

## Biblical grounding

- Genesis 3:1 is the opening move: the serpent's question casts doubt on God's word, recasting the wide permission of Genesis 2:16-17 as if God had withheld every tree. The doubt is planted by a question, before any denial is spoken.
- Genesis 3:2-3 is Eve's recounting of the command: she states the permission to eat of every tree of the garden, the prohibition of the one tree, and the death warning. Her recounting adds the phrase neither shall you touch it, which does not appear in the command as given in Genesis 2:16-17. The addition is noted here as her addition, established by direct textual comparison of the two passages, and for nothing more than the text supports.
- Genesis 3:4-5 is the serpent's contradiction and offer: the threatened death is denied outright, and being like God, knowing good and evil, is offered as what the prohibition withholds. Denial of the sanction and promise of elevation stand together in the same speech.
- Genesis 3:6 is the act in its interior and exterior order: the tree is seen to be good for food, a delight to the eyes, and desired to make one wise, and then she took and ate, and she gave to her husband who was with her, and he ate. The re-judging of the tree by desire precedes the hand taking the fruit in the text's own sequence.
- Genesis 3:7 is the immediate consequence inside the pair: their eyes are opened, they know that they are naked, and they sew fig leaves together and make loincloths. Opened eyes issue in shame and in a covering of their own making.
- Genesis 3:8-10 is hiding and the divine call: they hide themselves from the presence of the LORD God among the trees of the garden, God calls to the man, Where are you, and Adam confesses that he heard God's sound in the garden, was afraid because he was naked, and hid himself. Fear is confessed in Adam's own words.
- Genesis 3:11-13 is questioning and blame: under God's questions Adam answers that the woman whom God gave to be with him gave him the fruit, and Eve answers that the serpent deceived her and she ate. The answers state true facts in a form that shifts blame, Adam toward the woman and, by the phrase whom you gave, toward God, and Eve toward the serpent.
- Genesis 3:14-19 gives the judgments in order: judgment on the serpent, judgment concerning the woman, and judgment concerning the man, with toil, pain, thorns, sweat, and return to dust. Inside the serpent's judgment stands Genesis 3:15, the enmity between the serpent and the woman and between his offspring and her offspring, with the seed who will bruise the serpent's head and whose heel the serpent will bruise. The first promise of the seed is therefore located inside a sentence of judgment, not apart from it.
- Genesis 3:20-21 gives mercy inside the sentence: Adam names his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all living, and the LORD God makes garments of skins for Adam and his wife and clothes them. Life is named forward, and a covering better than fig leaves is given by God Himself.
- Genesis 3:22-24 is exile guarding the tree: the man is sent out of Eden lest he take also of the tree of life and live forever in his fallen state, the ground east of Eden is tilled, and cherubim with a flaming sword guard the way to the tree of life. Exile is both sentence and guard.
- Romans 5:12 is the canonical consequence read back over the whole chapter: sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned. This is canonical consequence, not a claim that Genesis 3 uses that later vocabulary.

## Doctrinal derivation

Premises. (1) The arrangement breached is the arrangement of BWM-IS-07: God initiated blessing, placement, calling, permission, command, promise, and warning before any human response (Genesis 1:28; 2:8, 15-18), and Adam stood upright, aided, and able to stand, yet unconfirmed (treatise axiom III.5). (2) The command and its sanction were clear as given (Genesis 2:16-17), and Genesis 3:2-3 shows the command known and recounted, with the one addition noted above. (3) The sequence in Genesis 3:6 places judgment and desire before the outward taking, so the narrative order itself supports an interior act before the exterior act. (4) Adam acts as head, so his breach cannot remain a private matter (Romans 5:12).

Consequences. Communion becomes rebellion without any change in God or in the command: what changes is the creature's judgment of God's word, first doubted (panel 1), then denied (panel 3), then displaced by desire's own verdict (panel 4). Shame, hiding, fear, and blame follow the act in that order and are its first fruits, before any sentence is pronounced (panels 6 to 8). Judgment, when it comes, is spoken by God in address to serpent, woman, and man in turn, and it carries both consequence and, in Genesis 3:15, promise (panel 9). Mercy is not postponed to a later chapter: naming, clothing, and the guarding of the tree of life all stand inside Genesis 3 itself (Genesis 3:20-24, in the Biblical grounding; the plate's panel 10 checklist closes on the promise that stands at the head of that mercy, Genesis 3:15).

Synthesis, labeled as synthesis. Two elements of the plate are synthesis and are carried at the grades stated. (a) The evil-will-before-evil-act framing (panels 4 and 5): Augustine states the order as a principle, that our first parents fell into open disobedience because already they were secretly corrupted, for the evil act had never been done had not an evil will preceded it, and that pride, the craving for undue exaltation, is the beginning of sin, as reported in treatise Part IV sec. 2. As a report of Augustine, City of God XIV.13 in that section, the framing stands at HIGH as a report. As this volume's framing of Genesis 3:6, it is synthesis at MEDIUM, joining Augustine's principle to the narrative sequence, and it is labeled as such in claim 08-4. (b) The lapsarian covenant of grace framing (panel 9): Genesis 3:15 as the point where the covenant of grace is set at the Fall and sealed by death (Hebrews 9:15) is JD's held position / synthesis at MEDIUM, per his ruling of 2026-11-13, as recorded in the abstract above.

Transmission, noted briefly. The inherited condition, the condition inherited before it is chosen, is BWM-IS-10's fuller subject. Treatise Part IV sec. 9 states its warrant at the level the sources support: the apostolic frame of Romans 5:12-21, Augustine's prose for the state that followed the fall in the Enchiridion and in De correptione et gratia, received at axiom IV.2, and Calvin's transmission clause that man's ruin brought with it the destruction of all his posterity (Institutes 3.23.8, as quoted in Part IV sec. 2). The mechanics of propagation are expressly left open in that section, with named controls standing at NOT YET CONSULTED, and this volume builds no mechanics of its own. Only the brief note belongs here, because Romans 5:12 is this volume's closing canonical consequence.

Objections and limits. The governing limit is that God is not the author of sin. The fall's fault is wholly man's: thesis T-IV.3 states that the fall was man's own act and fault, and its dependence on axiom V.3 is presupposed here, not argued here, since the standing of the fall under the decree belongs to the treatise's Part V locus. Adam might have stood if he chose, since it was only by his own will that he fell, as Calvin, Institutes 1.15.8, is quoted in Part IV sec. 2, HIGH as a report: mutability is not defect, and providence is not an excuse, because the fault clause stands entire and unsoftened. A second limit concerns the confessional boundary. The confessional boundary check for Westminster Confession ch. 6 stands in the treatise register, Part IV sec. 15, item (3), as an open check, not performed. This narrative therefore cites WCF 6.1-6.2 below as a confessional anchor with that caveat stated in full: HIGH as a report of the confession text only, and no claim is made here that the treatise's boundary check against that chapter has been completed. A third limit concerns Eve's addition in Genesis 3:3: the narrative records the addition by textual comparison and draws no conclusion about motive from it beyond what the comparison itself shows.
