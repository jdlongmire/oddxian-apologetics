### 7. Pore Pressure Literature Review
- **Report:** `20251217_PorePressure_LiteratureReview.md`
- **Result:** Mixed - partial support, significant challenges

**Supportive Findings:**
- Near-lithostatic pressures DO exist (drilling-confirmed in basins)
- Effective friction μ' < 0.1 documented at subduction zones globally
- Pore pressure ratios λ > 0.9 observed at decollements
- Thermal pressurization can drive friction to "near zero"
- Water thin-films confirmed critical to fault behavior

**Challenging Findings:**
- **Fault valving:** Slip INCREASES permeability → RELEASES pressure
- **Drainage timescale:** ~1 second at fault permeabilities
- **Scale gap:** Documented at fault-scale (km), not continent-scale (1000s km)
- **Transient vs. sustained:** Literature shows dynamic weakening, not static lubrication

**Core Problem Identified:**
- Model assumes: sustained near-lithostatic pressure enables continuous motion
- Literature shows: slip releases pressure through permeability increase
- The critic's "self-defeating" objection is SUBSTANTIATED by fault valving research

**Potential Refinements:**
1. Integrate thermal pressurization (dynamic pressure generation during slip)
2. Consider episodic motion model (slip → drain → recharge → slip)
3. Acknowledge model as research direction, not complete solution

### 8. Spongy-Model Architecture Clarification

### 9. Revised Literature Search - Correct Analogs
- **Submarine landslide hydroplaning:** Direct analog - water-saturated basal layer enables long run-out on gentle slopes
- **Seepage-supported sliding (Nature Comm 2023):** Flow ITSELF supports load via seepage forces - drainage doesn't kill lubrication
- **Liquefaction mechanics:** Effective stress → 0 when pore pressure rises
- **Consolidation theory:** Rate depends on permeability vs. drainage

**Critical Finding:**
> "Liquefaction can occur under drained conditions... seepage forces exert force on soil grains, supporting their weight"

This addresses the "self-defeating" objection - continuous flow provides continuous support.
