# What I Have Come to Realize

*[This post was originally published as a reflection piece with accompanying imagery.]*

The journey of faith is not a straight line. It winds through doubt, through questions, through seasons of certainty and seasons of wrestling. What I have come to realize is that this wrestling is not the enemy of faith; it is often its refiner.

The questions we bring to God are not threats to Him. He is not intimidated by our doubts or unsettled by our struggles. The God who spoke galaxies into existence can handle our honest inquiries. What He asks is that we bring them to Him rather than running from Him.

I have come to realize that faith is not the absence of questions but the presence of trust in the One who holds the answers. It is not believing despite the evidence but believing because the evidence, rightly considered, points somewhere. It points to a Mind behind the cosmos, a Word that gives meaning to words, a Logos that grounds all logic.

I have come to realize that the world's alternatives to Christianity do not actually offer more freedom, more meaning, or more hope. They offer less. Materialism gives you a universe that doesn't care. Relativism gives you a morality that can't bind. Nihilism gives you a life that doesn't matter. Only in Christ do all the pieces come together: truth, goodness, beauty, and hope.

I have come to realize that the gospel is not just true; it is the truest thing. It explains why we hunger for justice in an unjust world. It explains why we long for permanence in a world of decay. It explains why we crave meaning in a universe that, without God, offers none.

What I have come to realize is what the church has always known: Jesus Christ is Lord. And that changes everything.
